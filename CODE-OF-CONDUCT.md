## 1. Purpose

Framing Creativity Lab aims to provide a constructive, respectful, and intellectually open environment for research, experimentation, development, and creative collaboration.

The Lab brings together different disciplines, technical backgrounds, creative practices, and perspectives. Meaningful collaboration depends not only on technical standards, but also on the way people communicate, exchange ideas, challenge assumptions, and work through disagreements.

This Code of Conduct establishes the behavioral expectations that apply to everyone interacting with the Lab.

Its purpose is to:

- Encourage respectful and constructive collaboration.
- Maintain an environment open to different perspectives and disciplines.
- Support rigorous discussion without personal hostility.
- Establish clear boundaries for acceptable behavior.
- Provide a consistent approach to reporting and addressing misconduct.
- Protect contributors, participants, maintainers, and collaborators from harassment, intimidation, and abuse.

This Code applies regardless of professional status, experience, technical expertise, or contribution history.

---

## 2. Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to all spaces and activities officially associated with Framing Creativity Lab, including:

- Public GitHub repositories.
- Issues and pull requests.
- Code reviews and technical discussions.
- Research proposals and experiment submissions.
- Documentation and collaborative writing.
- Project demonstrations and public presentations.
- Official communication channels.
- Lab-related online meetings or events.
- Other spaces where an individual is formally representing the Lab.

The Code applies whether an interaction takes place publicly or privately, online or in person, provided that it is connected to Lab activities.

It also applies to maintainers and contributors when they represent the Lab or exercise authority within its projects.

---

## 3. Guiding Principles

The Lab is built around the following principles.

### 3.1 Respect

Treat people with dignity, regardless of their background, experience, identity, professional position, or personal circumstances.

Disagreement is acceptable. Disrespect is not.

### 3.2 Intellectual Openness

Encourage the exchange of ideas, critical thinking, experimentation, and constructive questioning.

No contributor should be expected to agree with every proposal or interpretation.

### 3.3 Constructive Criticism

Challenge ideas, methods, assumptions, and technical decisions without attacking the individuals presenting them.

Criticism should be relevant, proportionate, and supported by reasoning whenever possible.

### 3.4 Curiosity and Learning

Recognize that the Lab is a space for exploration.

Mistakes, incomplete experiments, unsuccessful prototypes, and changing perspectives are part of the research and development process.

### 3.5 Accountability

Everyone is responsible for their conduct and its impact on others.

When mistakes occur, participants are expected to acknowledge them, engage constructively, and make reasonable efforts to correct their behavior.

### 3.6 Transparency

Communicate expectations, decisions, and project limitations clearly.

Do not use ambiguity, hidden criteria, or personal influence to undermine fair collaboration.

---

## 4. Expected Behavior

All participants are expected to:

- Communicate respectfully and professionally.
- Assume good faith while remaining open to evidence and clarification.
- Listen to different perspectives.
- Provide constructive and relevant feedback.
- Respect the time and contributions of others.
- Acknowledge sources, authorship, and intellectual contributions.
- Respect intellectual property and licensing requirements.
- Recognize the limits of their own expertise.
- Accept reasonable criticism and review.
- Disagree without becoming hostile or personal.
- Respect project scope, technical decisions, and established contribution processes.
- Protect private, confidential, and sensitive information.
- Raise concerns through appropriate channels.
- Contribute to an environment where people can participate without intimidation or harassment.

Participants should also be mindful of different communication styles, levels of technical knowledge, cultural contexts, and language abilities.

Clarity is more valuable than performative expertise.

## 5. Unacceptable Behavior

The following behaviors are not acceptable within Lab spaces or activities.

### 5.1 Harassment and Discrimination

- Personal attacks, insults, or degrading remarks.
- Harassment based on identity or personal characteristics.
- Sexist, racist, homophobic, transphobic, or otherwise discriminatory conduct.
- Unwelcome sexual attention or inappropriate personal remarks.
- Deliberate humiliation or intimidation.
- Repeated unwanted contact after a request to stop.

### 5.2 Abuse of Authority

- Using administrative or technical privileges to intimidate others.
- Threatening contributors for expressing disagreement.
- Unreasonably obstructing participation through personal hostility.
- Using influence within the Lab to retaliate against criticism.
- Misrepresenting decisions or authority to pressure participants.

### 5.3 Disruptive Conduct

- Repeatedly derailing discussions through hostile or irrelevant behavior.
- Deliberately provoking or escalating conflict.
- Publishing private disputes to intimidate or embarrass individuals.
- Repeatedly ignoring reasonable communication boundaries.
- Flooding discussions with abusive, misleading, or disruptive content.

### 5.4 Misrepresentation and Intellectual Misconduct

- Presenting another person's work as one's own.
- Knowingly misrepresenting another contributor's statements or work.
- Deliberately concealing material conflicts of interest.
- Falsifying research findings, test results, or technical evidence.
- Claiming authorship or ownership without a legitimate basis.
- Using contributions outside their authorized scope.

### 5.5 Privacy and Confidentiality Violations

- Publishing personal information without authorization.
- Disclosing private conversations without an appropriate reason or permission.
- Exposing credentials, private project material, or confidential research.
- Sharing private files, screenshots, or identifying information to intimidate or harm someone.
- Accessing or distributing information beyond one's authorized permissions.

### 5.6 Retaliation

Retaliation against someone who reports misconduct, participates in a review, or raises a concern in good faith is prohibited.

This includes threats, intimidation, exclusion, harassment, or deliberate interference with legitimate participation.

Knowingly false or malicious reports may also be considered misconduct. However, an allegation that cannot be substantiated is not automatically a malicious report.

## 6. Differences of Opinion and Technical Disagreement

The Lab encourages rigorous debate.

Technical decisions, research interpretations, creative directions, and methodological choices may be challenged.

Participants are encouraged to:

- Explain their reasoning.
- Provide relevant evidence.
- Distinguish facts from assumptions.
- Identify trade-offs and limitations.
- Consider alternative approaches.
- Accept that a discussion may end without complete agreement.

The following distinction is fundamental:

| Constructive disagreement | Unacceptable conduct |
|---|---|
| Challenging an architectural decision | Attacking the person who proposed it |
| Questioning research methodology | Dismissing someone through humiliation |
| Requesting evidence | Using intimidation to silence discussion |
| Disagreeing with a creative direction | Discrediting someone's identity or ability |
| Rejecting a proposal with clear reasoning | Retaliating against its author |

Not every disagreement is a conflict, and not every rejected proposal represents unfair treatment.

The maintainer retains responsibility for project decisions, but that authority does not exempt them from the expectations defined in this Code.

## 7. Maintainer Responsibilities

The maintainer is responsible for applying this Code within the Lab's spaces and activities.

This includes:

- Communicating behavioral expectations.
- Reviewing reported incidents in good faith.
- Taking reasonable action when misconduct is identified.
- Protecting reporters and affected individuals from retaliation.
- Applying measures proportionately.
- Avoiding unnecessary disclosure of sensitive information.
- Documenting significant enforcement decisions where appropriate.
- Reviewing the Code when the Lab's structure or community evolves.

The Lab is currently maintained through a founder-led governance model.

There is no formally established moderation committee, independent ombudsperson, or dedicated disciplinary body.

The maintainer should therefore recognize potential conflicts of interest when a report concerns their own conduct or a decision in which they are directly involved.

Where appropriate and feasible, an independent person may be invited to review the matter with the agreement of the relevant parties.

If independent review is not available, the limitation should be acknowledged rather than presenting the process as independent.

## 8. Reporting a Concern

Anyone who experiences or witnesses conduct that may violate this Code is encouraged to report it.

Reports may concern:

- Harassment or discrimination.
- Threatening or abusive behavior.
- Retaliation.
- Misuse of authority.
- Privacy or confidentiality violations.
- Repeated disruptive conduct.
- Other behavior that materially affects the safety or integrity of Lab activities.

### 8.1 Reporting Channel

Reports should be submitted privately to the maintainer through a private communication method explicitly published by Framing Creativity Lab.

A public GitHub issue, pull request, or discussion thread should not be used for sensitive allegations or personal information.

The Lab must publish an accessible private reporting method before treating this reporting process as operational.

No unpublished email address or private contact channel should be assumed.

### 8.2 Information to Include

A report should include, where possible:

- A description of the incident.
- The relevant date or approximate timeframe.
- The space or project where it occurred.
- The individuals involved, where known.
- Relevant messages, links, screenshots, or other evidence.
- Any previous attempts to resolve the situation.
- The impact of the behavior.
- Any immediate concerns about safety, privacy, or retaliation.

Reports should be factual and proportionate.

A reporter does not need to provide every detail immediately, especially where doing so could expose them to further harm.

### 8.3 Confidentiality

Reports will be handled with discretion.

Information should be shared only to the extent reasonably necessary to assess and address the concern.

However, absolute confidentiality cannot be guaranteed. Some information may need to be disclosed to understand the incident, provide a fair opportunity to respond, or comply with applicable legal obligations.

The identity of a reporter should not be disclosed unnecessarily.

---

## 9. Review and Assessment Process

Reports should be assessed through a proportionate and reasonable process.

### Stage 1 — Initial Review

The maintainer reviews the report to understand its nature, scope, urgency, and potential impact.

Additional information may be requested where necessary.

### Stage 2 — Assessment

The maintainer considers:

- The reported behavior.
- Available evidence.
- The context of the interaction.
- The impact on affected individuals.
- Whether the behavior appears intentional, repeated, or particularly serious.
- Whether immediate protective measures are necessary.

### Stage 3 — Response

Where appropriate, the people involved may be invited to provide relevant context.

The process should avoid unnecessary confrontation or direct contact between parties where that could increase risk.

### Stage 4 — Decision

The maintainer determines whether the Code has been violated and whether corrective or disciplinary action is appropriate.

Decisions should be proportionate to the seriousness and context of the conduct.

### Stage 5 — Follow-up

Where appropriate, the maintainer communicates the outcome to the relevant parties and considers whether additional safeguards or restrictions are needed.

The Lab does not guarantee a fixed response time or a formal appeal process.

## 10. Enforcement Measures

Violations may result in one or more of the following measures, depending on their severity and context.

### 10.1 Informal Guidance

A participant may receive a private request to clarify or correct behavior that appears inconsistent with this Code.

### 10.2 Formal Warning

A participant may receive a clear warning describing the behavior, the relevant expectations, and the consequences of further violations.

### 10.3 Content Moderation

Inappropriate content may be edited, hidden, removed, or otherwise moderated where the relevant platform and permissions allow.

### 10.4 Temporary Restriction

Access to specific discussions, repositories, collaboration spaces, or activities may be temporarily restricted.

### 10.5 Removal of Access

A participant may lose repository access, collaboration privileges, or permission to participate in Lab activities.

### 10.6 Permanent Exclusion

For serious or repeated misconduct, a participant may be permanently excluded from Lab spaces and activities.

### 10.7 External Reporting

Where appropriate, suspected illegal activity, credible threats, or other serious matters may be referred to the relevant platform or competent authorities.

The Lab is not a substitute for emergency services, legal counsel, or law enforcement.

## 11. Proportionality and Serious Incidents

Not every violation requires the same response.

The maintainer should consider:

- Severity and potential harm.
- Frequency and persistence.
- Intent and circumstances.
- Previous conduct.
- Willingness to acknowledge and correct the behavior.
- Risk of recurrence.
- Impact on affected individuals and the wider Lab environment.

Minor misunderstandings may be resolved through clarification.

Serious harassment, threats, deliberate exposure of private information, retaliation, or repeated abuse may justify immediate restrictions or exclusion without prior informal warnings.

Protective measures may be taken before an investigation is complete when reasonably necessary to reduce an immediate risk.

---

## 12. Good-Faith Reporting and Fair Treatment

The Lab encourages people to report concerns without fear of retaliation.

A report made in good faith should not result in punishment simply because it cannot be substantiated.

At the same time, the process should respect the rights and dignity of all parties.

This means:

- Reports should be assessed on their substance.
- Conclusions should not be predetermined.
- Relevant context should be considered.
- Personal disagreement should not be treated as proof of misconduct.
- Confidential information should be protected.
- Enforcement should be proportionate.
- Retaliation should not be tolerated.

The purpose of this process is to maintain a healthy collaborative environment, not to suppress legitimate criticism or differences of opinion.

---

## 13. Conflicts Involving the Maintainer

The same behavioral expectations apply to the maintainer.

A person raising a concern about the maintainer should not be expected to treat the maintainer's position as a reason to remain silent.

Where the concern involves the maintainer directly:

- The conflict of interest should be recognized.
- The maintainer should avoid presenting their own assessment as independent review.
- An independent reviewer may be invited where feasible and appropriate.
- Relevant platform reporting mechanisms may be used where applicable.
- Any limitations in the review process should be communicated honestly.

Because the Lab does not currently operate an independent governance or complaints body, it cannot guarantee independent adjudication of every report involving the maintainer.

This limitation does not remove the obligation to take concerns seriously.

---

## 14. Application to Contributors and Collaborators

By participating in Lab activities, contributors and collaborators are expected to follow this Code.

This includes people submitting:

- Code.
- Research.
- Experiments.
- Technical documentation.
- Creative work.
- Design proposals.
- Reviews and feedback.
- Other forms of collaboration.

Acceptance of a contribution does not exempt its author from this Code.

Likewise, disagreement with a maintainer decision does not justify harassment, retaliation, or abusive behavior.

Project-specific contribution requirements may add further expectations but must not undermine the principles established here.

## 16. Policy Review and Updates

This Code of Conduct may evolve as Framing Creativity Lab develops new projects, welcomes additional contributors, or establishes new collaboration structures.

Updates should preserve its foundational principles:

- Respect.
- Inclusion.
- Intellectual openness.
- Accountability.
- Fair treatment.
- Transparency.
- Responsible collaboration.

The maintainer is responsible for approving and documenting revisions.

## 17. Acknowledgment

Framing Creativity Lab is built on the belief that meaningful work emerges from both individual perspectives and constructive exchange.

A strong collaborative environment does not require uniform thinking.

It requires mutual respect, clear boundaries, intellectual honesty, and the ability to challenge ideas without diminishing people.

**Think freely. Challenge constructively. Collaborate responsibly.**
