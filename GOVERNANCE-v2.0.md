## 1. Purpose

Framing Creativity Lab operates as an independent, founder-led research and development environment.

This document defines how the Lab is directed, how responsibilities and decision-making authority are assigned, and how projects, repositories, collaborations, and material risks are governed.

Its purpose is to:

- Preserve the Lab's identity, independence, and strategic direction.
- Establish clear responsibility for decisions and actions.
- Define the current governance model and the limits of delegated authority.
- Provide consistent governance for projects from intake through publication, maintenance, or closure.
- Establish expectations for contributors, collaborators, and external providers.
- Protect intellectual property, confidential information, privacy, and security.
- Support accountable decisions without introducing unnecessary administrative complexity.

Governance should enable responsible experimentation. It should not become an obstacle to it.

## 2. Scope and Governance Framework

### 2.1 Scope

This document applies to the governance of Framing Creativity Lab, its public repositories, its projects, and activities carried out under the Lab's identity.

It addresses organizational responsibility, decision-making, project oversight, access, collaboration, intellectual property considerations, risk, and continuity.

It does not replace detailed technical, research, security, contribution, or publication procedures established in the Lab's other policies.

### 2.2 Relationship with the Lab Charter

`LAB-CHARTER-v2.0.md` establishes the Lab's foundational identity, mission, principles, and broad institutional direction.

This Governance document translates those foundations into practical responsibilities, decision rights, and operating arrangements.

### 2.3 Policy-Specific Authority

Where a subject is governed by a dedicated policy, that policy provides the detailed requirements for that subject. In particular:

- `PROJECT-LIFECYCLE-v2.0.md` owns the canonical project lifecycle stages and Project Status definitions.
- `QUALITY-STANDARDS-v2.0.md` owns the Technical Maturity and Operational Readiness definitions.
- `PUBLICATION-POLICY-v2.0.md` owns Publication Classification, Publication Status, and publication authorization requirements.
- `SECURITY-v2.0.md` governs security reporting and vulnerability handling.
- `CONTRIBUTING-v2.0.md` governs contribution procedures and contributor workflow.
- `CODE-OF-CONDUCT-v2.0.md` governs expected conduct and conduct-related procedures.
- `RESEARCH-METHODOLOGY-v2.0.md` governs research design and experimental methodology.

This document may refer to those policies but must not create competing vocabularies or override their detailed requirements.

### 2.4 Applicable Rights and Obligations

This governance framework does not override applicable law, contracts, third-party terms, intellectual property rights, or project-specific agreements.

Where a legal, contractual, licensing, or security restriction applies, the Lab must respect that restriction regardless of an internal governance decision.

## 3. Governance Principles

### 3.1 Independence

Framing Creativity Lab is an independent initiative. Its research direction, publication decisions, and operating principles are maintained independently unless a specific written agreement establishes otherwise.

Participation in the Lab does not imply affiliation with an external organization, company, institution, or commercial project.

### 3.2 Founder-Led Responsibility

The Lab currently operates under the direction of its founder and maintainer, J-ART.

Overall governance authority remains identifiable and centralized unless a specific responsibility or authority is explicitly delegated and recorded.

### 3.3 Intellectual Freedom

The Lab encourages exploration across artificial intelligence, creative technology, software engineering, digital experiences, and related disciplines.

Ideas and proposals should be considered on their relevance, potential value, feasibility, evidence, and alignment with the Lab's principles rather than on disciplinary boundaries alone.

### 3.4 Accountability

Material decisions must have an identifiable decision-maker. Responsibilities, approvals, and delegated authority should be clear enough to establish who is accountable for an action.

### 3.5 Proportionality

Governance requirements should reflect a project's scale, complexity, intended use, maturity, and risk.

A small exploratory experiment does not require the same level of review as a publicly deployed system handling personal or sensitive information. Proportionality does not permit the omission of mandatory legal, security, privacy, or publication requirements.

### 3.6 Transparency with Boundaries

The Lab aims to make its public scope, policies, project descriptions, and contribution expectations understandable.

Transparency does not override confidentiality, privacy, security, intellectual property rights, or contractual restrictions.

### 3.7 Evidence-Based Decisions

Decisions about technical maturity, operational readiness, research findings, and public claims should be supported by evidence proportionate to the claim being made.

Intention, visual polish, public availability, or a successful demonstration must not be treated as proof of validation or production suitability.

### 3.8 Responsible Evolution

The governance model may evolve as the Lab's activities, resources, or participation change. Changes to authority or organizational structure must be intentional, documented, and consistent with the Lab Charter.

## 4. Organizational Structure and Roles

### 4.1 Current Governance Model

Framing Creativity Lab currently operates as a founder-led initiative. The present operating model includes the following roles:

| Role | Current position or function |
|---|---|
| Founder and Maintainer | J-ART; holds overall governance responsibility |
| Project Lead | A person explicitly assigned responsibility for a defined project or workstream |
| Contributor | A person whose proposed contribution has been accepted under the applicable terms |
| Collaborator | A person or organization participating in a specifically agreed activity |
| External Provider | A third-party service, platform, tool, model, or infrastructure provider |

This table describes functional roles. It does not imply that the Lab has employees, departments, a board, a formal committee, or an established advisory body.

A role title does not create authority beyond the scope explicitly assigned to it.

### 4.2 Founder and Maintainer

The founder and maintainer holds overall responsibility for the Lab's identity, direction, governance, and public integrity.

Responsibilities include:

- Maintaining the Lab's mission, principles, and strategic direction.
- Approving material changes to the governance framework.
- Deciding whether projects are accepted into the Lab's portfolio.
- Assigning or withdrawing project responsibilities.
- Maintaining the official policies and standards.
- Approving publication and withdrawal decisions.
- Managing repository administration and access, subject to platform controls.
- Addressing material security, privacy, confidentiality, and governance concerns.
- Determining priorities in light of available resources.
- Authorizing official representation of the Lab.

The maintainer may seek advice or delegate defined tasks. Overall governance authority remains with the maintainer unless formally changed through an approved governance revision.

### 4.3 Project Lead

A project lead may be assigned to coordinate a defined project or workstream.

The assignment should identify, as appropriate:

- The project or workstream covered.
- The responsibilities being delegated.
- Decisions the project lead may make independently.
- Decisions reserved for the maintainer.
- Relevant access permissions.
- Reporting or documentation expectations.
- The duration or review conditions of the assignment.

A project lead may coordinate implementation and recommend project decisions. The title does not, by itself, grant authority to change Lab policy, approve public release, transfer intellectual property, grant repository administration, or represent the Lab externally.

### 4.4 Contributors

Contributors may propose, develop, review, document, test, or improve work within an agreed scope.

Contribution does not automatically grant:

- Ownership of the Lab or its identity.
- Repository administration rights.
- General decision-making authority.
- Permission to represent the Lab externally.
- Rights to use or disclose unrelated confidential material.
- Permission for the Lab to use, distribute, or relicense a contribution beyond the rights expressly established by an applicable licence or written agreement.

The rights and permissions applicable to a contribution must be clear before it is accepted for incorporation or distribution. Submission alone must not be treated as a transfer or grant of rights.

### 4.5 Collaborators

Collaborators participate in specific activities under an agreed scope. Collaboration may include research, technical development, creative experimentation, design, prototyping, testing, evaluation, or documentation.

Before substantive work begins, the parties should clarify the scope, responsibilities, attribution, confidentiality, intellectual property, access, and publication conditions that apply.

Collaboration does not automatically establish a partnership, employment relationship, agency relationship, joint ownership arrangement, or authority to bind another party.

### 4.6 External Providers

External providers operate services, platforms, models, libraries, infrastructure, or tools used by the Lab.

Their responsibilities and obligations are governed by their applicable terms and agreements. Use of a provider does not imply endorsement, partnership, or formal affiliation.

The Lab should evaluate relevant provider terms, licensing, data handling, security, availability, cost, and dependency risks before adopting a service for material work.

## 5. Decision-Making Authority

Decision-making authority is assigned according to the nature and impact of a decision. Unless a specific delegation is recorded, authority remains with the founder and maintainer.

### 5.1 Strategic Decisions

Strategic decisions affect the Lab's identity, mission, independence, research direction, or long-term structure.

Examples include:

- Changes to the Lab's mission, objectives, or foundational principles.
- Changes to its principal research domains or public identity.
- Establishment of a major strategic initiative.
- Changes to the governance model or distribution of authority.
- Formal affiliation, partnership, or institutional arrangements.

**Decision authority:** Founder and Maintainer.

### 5.2 Operational Decisions

Operational decisions concern the day-to-day administration and functioning of the Lab.

Examples include repository organization, documentation maintenance, tooling, workflows, access permissions, and internal priorities.

**Decision authority:** Founder and Maintainer, or a person acting within an explicitly documented delegation.

### 5.3 Project Decisions

Project decisions concern the scope, direction, resources, implementation, and continuation of an individual project.

Examples include:

- Accepting a project proposal.
- Defining or materially changing project scope.
- Selecting technical approaches.
- Establishing milestones and evaluation criteria.
- Approving material architecture changes.
- Assigning project responsibilities.
- Pausing, archiving, or discontinuing a project.

**Decision authority:** Founder and Maintainer, informed by the project lead, contributors, or collaborators where appropriate. A project lead may make only those decisions included in an explicit delegation.

### 5.4 Publication Decisions

Publication decisions determine whether specified code, documentation, research, assets, demonstrations, or other material may be made publicly available under the Lab's identity.

**Decision authority:** Founder and Maintainer, unless a specific publication authority has been explicitly delegated in writing.

Every release must comply with `PUBLICATION-POLICY-v2.0.md`, applicable rights and licences, security and privacy requirements, confidentiality obligations, and relevant legal restrictions.

A completed project, accepted contribution, merged change, or public repository location does not by itself constitute publication approval.

### 5.5 Security, Privacy, and Confidentiality Decisions

These decisions may concern vulnerability handling, credential exposure, access restrictions, data protection, confidentiality incidents, suspension of a service, or withdrawal of material.

**Decision authority:** Founder and Maintainer, subject to applicable law, contractual duties, and relevant third-party procedures.

Where immediate protective action is reasonably necessary to reduce a material risk, the maintainer may restrict access, suspend affected activity, or initiate containment before a full review is complete. The action and its rationale should be recorded when appropriate.

### 5.6 Reserved Decisions

Unless formally amended, the following remain reserved to the founder and maintainer:

- Changes to the Lab Charter or governance structure.
- Appointment or removal of people with delegated governance authority.
- Granting or transferring administrative control of official repositories.
- Approval or withdrawal of public releases under the Lab's identity.
- Approval of formal partnerships or commitments made on behalf of the Lab.
- Decisions involving material intellectual property, confidentiality, or legal exposure.

## 6. Decision-Making Process

The Lab uses a proportionate process. Routine reversible decisions may be made directly; decisions with material consequences should have a clear rationale and accountable decision-maker.

### 6.1 General Process

As appropriate to the decision:

1. **Identify** the question, request, risk, or decision required.
2. **Establish context** by gathering the relevant technical, research, operational, legal, and project information.
3. **Assess options** and consider consequences, dependencies, uncertainty, and alternatives.
4. **Consult** affected contributors or collaborators where their expertise or interests are relevant.
5. **Decide** through the person or role holding the applicable authority.
6. **Record** the decision when it materially affects scope, rights, risk, resources, authority, or publication.
7. **Reassess** when new evidence or changed circumstances make the original decision unreliable or inappropriate.

### 6.2 Consultation

Consultation is encouraged where a decision affects contributors, collaborators, users, or the direction of a project.

Consultation is normally advisory. It does not transfer decision-making authority unless an explicit arrangement states otherwise.

Where a decision materially affects a person's contribution, rights, access, or obligations, the relevant terms and applicable procedures should be considered before the decision is finalized.

### 6.3 Decision Criteria

Relevant criteria may include:

- Alignment with the Lab's mission and principles.
- Research, technical, or creative value.
- Evidence and feasibility.
- Quality and maintainability.
- Security, privacy, and confidentiality.
- Intellectual property, licensing, and attribution.
- Operational readiness and support requirements.
- Resource availability and cost.
- External dependencies and continuity risks.
- Ethical, legal, and societal implications.

The criteria and their relative importance should reflect the nature and impact of the decision.

### 6.4 Recording Decisions

Material decisions may be recorded in project documentation, issues, review records, Architecture Decision Records, research reports, release records, or policy revision history.

The record should identify the decision, its date, the responsible authority, the rationale, and any material conditions or follow-up actions.

Sensitive information must not be placed in a public record. Use an appropriate restricted location where a confidential record is necessary.

## 7. Project Governance and Canonical Project Fields

Each project should be governed and documented at a level appropriate to its purpose, complexity, impact, and risk.

### 7.1 Project Intake and Definition

A project may originate from a research question, technical exploration, creative concept, prototype proposal, documented problem, or opportunity aligned with the Lab's mission.

Before significant development or a publication decision, the project should have a sufficiently clear record of:

- Project name and purpose.
- Objectives, research question, or intended outcome.
- Scope, boundaries, and intended use.
- Expected deliverables.
- Responsible maintainer or project lead.
- Principal technologies and dependencies, where relevant.
- Data, privacy, security, and confidentiality considerations.
- Intellectual property and licensing considerations.
- Relevant risks and known limitations.
- Current lifecycle and assessment fields, where applicable.

A small experiment may use a concise record. More complex or higher-risk work requires proportionately fuller documentation.

### 7.2 Lifecycle Stage

**Lifecycle Stage** identifies the project's position in the Lab's project workflow.

The nine canonical stages are owned by `PROJECT-LIFECYCLE-v2.0.md`:

1. Idea and Intake
2. Proposal
3. Discovery and Feasibility
4. Experimentation and Development
5. Validation and Evaluation
6. Publication Review
7. Publication and Release
8. Maintenance and Evolution
9. Archival or Discontinuation

This list is reproduced here for orientation. `PROJECT-LIFECYCLE-v2.0.md` remains the authoritative source for stage definitions, activities, and exit criteria.

A project may revisit a stage, repeat an investigation, omit a stage that is not relevant, or stop before publication. The lifecycle is not a requirement to publish every project.

### 7.3 Project Status

**Project Status** describes the project's current state. It is distinct from Lifecycle Stage and must use the canonical vocabulary owned by `PROJECT-LIFECYCLE-v2.0.md`:

| Project Status | Definition |
|---|---|
| Proposed | An idea or project proposal awaiting evaluation |
| Concept | An early-stage concept under exploration |
| Experimental | An active experiment or technical exploration |
| Prototype | A demonstrable implementation used to evaluate an approach |
| Development | An actively evolving implementation |
| Validation | A project undergoing structured testing or evaluation |
| Published | An approved project or result available publicly |
| Maintenance | A published project receiving ongoing updates |
| Archived | A project retained for reference without active development |
| Discontinued | A project formally closed and no longer intended for development |

A project may use a subset of these statuses. The selected value must describe the current state, not a desired future state.

### 7.4 Technical Maturity

**Technical Maturity** assesses the maturity of the technical work. Its canonical values are owned by `QUALITY-STANDARDS-v2.0.md`:

- `Not Assessed`
- `Exploratory`
- `Prototype`
- `Validated`

The value must be supported by the evidence and scope of assessment. It is not a lifecycle stage, a Project Status, a publication decision, or a claim of production readiness.

### 7.5 Operational Readiness

**Operational Readiness** assesses the context and conditions for operating the work. Its canonical values are owned by `QUALITY-STANDARDS-v2.0.md`:

- `Not Assessed`
- `Research or Experimental Use Only`
- `Demonstration Only`
- `Limited Operational Use`
- `Production-Oriented`

A readiness value must reflect the actual operating context, safeguards, responsibilities, and evidence. In particular, `Production-Oriented` must not be inferred from a successful demonstration, a public deployment, a polished interface, or technical maturity alone.

### 7.6 Publication Classification

**Publication Classification** determines the applicable level of publication scrutiny and restriction. Its canonical values are owned by `PUBLICATION-POLICY-v2.0.md`:

| Classification | Meaning |
|---|---|
| P0 | Public-ready |
| P1 | Controlled publication; additional checks or conditions apply |
| P2 | Restricted review; material must remain unpublished until concerns are resolved and reassessed |
| P3 | Non-publishable; excluded from the public publication process |

The full requirements and examples for each classification are defined in `PUBLICATION-POLICY-v2.0.md`. Classification is not a measure of technical maturity or project status.

### 7.7 Publication Status

**Publication Status** records the current review state or publication decision. Its canonical values are owned by `PUBLICATION-POLICY-v2.0.md`:

- `PENDING` — review or decision is not complete.
- `APPROVED` — publication has been authorized for the defined scope, subject to any recorded conditions.
- `REJECTED` — publication has not been authorized.
- `WITHDRAWN` — a previous approval or publication authorization has been withdrawn.

`APPROVED` does not itself mean that a release has already been made public. Publication must be carried out through the approved release process. A project may also be withdrawn after publication where the applicable policy requires it.

### 7.8 Keep the Fields Independent

The project fields answer different questions and must not be substituted for one another:

| Field | Question answered | Authoritative document |
|---|---|---|
| Lifecycle Stage | Where is the project in the workflow? | `PROJECT-LIFECYCLE-v2.0.md` |
| Project Status | What is the current state of the project? | `PROJECT-LIFECYCLE-v2.0.md` |
| Technical Maturity | How mature is the technical work? | `QUALITY-STANDARDS-v2.0.md` |
| Operational Readiness | In what operating context is the work suitable? | `QUALITY-STANDARDS-v2.0.md` |
| Publication Classification | What level of publication scrutiny or restriction applies? | `PUBLICATION-POLICY-v2.0.md` |
| Publication Status | What is the current publication review state or decision? | `PUBLICATION-POLICY-v2.0.md` |

A project record should assign each relevant field independently. One value must not be inferred solely from another.

## 8. Project Review, Change, and Closure

### 8.1 Project Review

The maintainer or an explicitly assigned project lead should review material changes to a project's purpose, scope, architecture, data handling, dependencies, risk, or intended use.

A material change may require renewed technical evaluation, risk assessment, or publication review.

### 8.2 Pausing a Project

A project may be paused when resources are unavailable, assumptions require further investigation, a dependency becomes unsuitable, or unresolved risks prevent responsible continuation.

The project record should be updated where the pause materially changes its status, expected maintenance, or public representation.

### 8.3 Archival and Discontinuation

Archival and discontinuation must follow `PROJECT-LIFECYCLE-v2.0.md` and applicable publication requirements.

A project that is no longer actively maintained must not continue to be represented as actively maintained. Where appropriate, documentation should identify its limitations, support expectations, and closure state.

### 8.4 Withdrawal

A public release may need to be withdrawn or restricted because of security, privacy, legal, licensing, confidentiality, or material quality concerns.

The maintainer should coordinate withdrawal actions under `PUBLICATION-POLICY-v2.0.md` and `SECURITY-v2.0.md`, as applicable. Public explanations should be accurate but must not disclose additional sensitive information.

## 9. Repository and Access Governance

Repository permissions must reflect actual responsibilities and follow the principle of least privilege.

### 9.1 Access Levels

Access may include read, triage, write, maintain, or administrator permissions, subject to the repository platform's available controls.

Not every contributor requires direct write access. Access should be limited to what a person needs for the agreed activity.

### 9.2 Approval of Access

The founder and maintainer approves access to official Lab repositories and associated resources unless a specific access-administration responsibility has been delegated.

Access decisions may consider project responsibilities, contribution needs, security, confidentiality, and the sensitivity of the relevant resources.

### 9.3 Review and Removal

Access should be reviewed when responsibilities change, a collaboration ends, a person no longer requires access, a project closes, or a security concern arises.

Permissions and credentials should not remain active without a legitimate operational reason. Where access is removed for security reasons, appropriate records should be retained without exposing sensitive details publicly.

### 9.4 Administrative Control

Administrative privileges remain under the control of the founder and maintainer unless explicitly delegated.

Delegation of administrative access must identify its scope and must not be interpreted as a transfer of ownership or overall governance authority.

## 10. Intellectual Property, Contributions, and Confidentiality

### 10.1 Ownership of the Lab

The Lab's identity, name, governance, and overall direction are maintained by its founder. Participation does not automatically confer ownership rights in the Lab or authority over its strategic decisions.

### 10.2 Rights in Contributions

A contribution remains subject to the rights held by its author and to any applicable licence or written agreement.

Submission, review, acceptance, or inclusion in a public repository must not be treated as an implied transfer of ownership or an unrestricted permission to use, distribute, modify, or relicense the contribution.

Before a contribution is accepted for incorporation or distribution, the permissions required for the intended use must be established clearly. Where those permissions are uncertain, the contribution must not be merged or published until the matter is resolved.

This document does not create a general contribution licence. The applicable rights must be established through the relevant project licence, contribution terms, or separate written agreement.

### 10.3 Third-Party Material

Contributors and project leads must identify relevant third-party code, models, datasets, documentation, media, and other assets and consider their applicable licences, terms, attribution requirements, and usage restrictions.

Material with unclear ownership, incompatible terms, or uncertain publication rights must be held from publication until the issue is resolved.

### 10.4 Project-Specific Agreements

A project or collaboration may require a separate agreement covering ownership, licensing, attribution, commercial use, confidentiality, data access, distribution, maintenance, or other rights and obligations.

Such an agreement must be explicit and appropriately authorized. Informal participation or silence must not be treated as consent to a transfer of rights or commercial exploitation.

### 10.5 Confidential and Proprietary Work

The public Lab repository must remain separate from private, proprietary, and confidential projects.

A related idea, general technique, or research interest does not authorize the publication of private implementation details, source code, prompts, workflows, datasets, credentials, assets, or internal documentation.

No participant may use the Lab as a channel to disclose material from another project without the necessary authorization.

### 10.6 Publication Boundary

Public accessibility is not equivalent to permission to reuse. All publication and distribution must follow `PUBLICATION-POLICY-v2.0.md` and the rights that actually apply to the material.

## 11. Collaboration Governance

### 11.1 Before Substantive Collaboration

Before substantive collaboration begins, the parties should clarify, as relevant:

- Purpose and expected outcomes.
- Scope and boundaries of the work.
- Responsibilities and decision rights.
- Communication and coordination arrangements.
- Attribution and recognition.
- Intellectual property and licensing.
- Confidentiality and data handling.
- Repository and resource access.
- Use of third-party tools and services.
- Publication, distribution, and commercial-use permissions.
- Duration, completion criteria, or termination conditions.

The level of formality should be proportionate to the commitments and risks. Significant commitments involving ownership, confidential information, financial obligations, personal data, or commercial use should be recorded in an appropriate agreement.

### 11.2 External Representation

Only a person explicitly authorized by the founder and maintainer may represent the Lab in an official capacity or make commitments on its behalf.

Contributors and collaborators must not imply that they speak for the Lab or announce a partnership, endorsement, or commitment without authorization.

### 11.3 Collaboration Boundaries

Collaboration does not automatically grant access to private repositories, internal documentation, confidential research, personal or sensitive data, proprietary assets, administrative credentials, or unrelated projects and infrastructure.

Access must be separately authorized and limited to the agreed scope.

### 11.4 Ending a Collaboration

When a collaboration ends, the parties should address outstanding work, access removal, confidential information, attribution, licences, and any continuing obligations under applicable agreements.

The end of a collaboration does not extinguish rights or obligations that continue under law, licence, or agreement.

## 12. Conflicts of Interest

A conflict of interest may arise where personal, professional, financial, or organizational interests could materially influence a decision or create a reasonable appearance of compromised judgment.

Examples may include:

- Evaluating a contribution that directly involves one's own commercial interests.
- Selecting a provider with whom the decision-maker has a financial relationship.
- Reviewing work where the reviewer has a direct competing interest.
- Using confidential Lab information for unrelated personal or commercial advantage.
- Making a publication decision that materially benefits an undisclosed external party.

A participant should disclose a relevant conflict when it may affect a material decision.

Depending on the circumstances, the maintainer may require additional disclosure, independent input, a change of reviewer, restricted access, or withdrawal from the relevant decision.

A conflict does not, by itself, establish misconduct. The purpose of disclosure is to support fair and informed decisions.

Where the maintainer has a direct conflict, the conflict and any proportionate safeguards should be recorded. Where no independent governance body exists, the Lab should not imply that an independent internal appeal mechanism is available.

## 13. Disagreements and Governance Concerns

Disagreements may concern project direction, technical architecture, research methods, creative interpretation, publication, attribution, ownership, contribution acceptance, access, or resource allocation.

The Lab encourages concerns to be raised clearly and addressed through evidence, relevant policy, and constructive discussion.

Where a material disagreement requires a formal decision, the process should:

1. Identify the question or decision being challenged.
2. Establish the relevant facts, records, and applicable terms.
3. Allow affected parties to provide relevant context.
4. Identify conflicts of interest and any safeguards needed.
5. Evaluate the applicable policy and constraints.
6. Confirm the decision-maker, decision, and rationale.
7. Record the outcome when appropriate.

Under the current founder-led model, the founder and maintainer retains final internal decision-making authority, subject to applicable law, contracts, licences, and third-party rights.

Concerns involving the maintainer require particular care. The Lab must not imply that an independent internal board or appeal process exists when it does not. Where appropriate, affected individuals may use relevant external, contractual, platform, or legal channels.

Conduct-related concerns must be handled under `CODE-OF-CONDUCT-v2.0.md`. Security vulnerabilities must be handled under `SECURITY-v2.0.md` rather than disclosed through an ordinary public discussion.

## 14. Security, Privacy, and Risk Governance

Security, privacy, and confidentiality are governance responsibilities as well as technical considerations.

Projects should identify and address risks proportionate to their functionality, data, intended users, exposure, and potential impact.

Governance review may include:

- Credential and secret management.
- Repository and environment access.
- Data minimization and permitted use.
- Personal and sensitive information.
- Third-party services and data processors.
- Dependency and supply-chain risks.
- AI-specific misuse, disclosure, and reliability risks.
- External interfaces and deployment exposure.
- Incident response and recovery.
- Publication and withdrawal implications.

Where a material risk cannot be reasonably addressed, the project may be restricted, delayed, modified, suspended, or withdrawn.

The maintainer should ensure that the relevant requirements in `SECURITY-v2.0.md`, `PUBLICATION-POLICY-v2.0.md`, `RESPONSIBLE-AI-v2.0.md`, and other applicable policies are considered.

No governance approval authorizes the collection, processing, retention, or disclosure of information without an appropriate legal and operational basis.

## 15. Resource and Priority Management

Framing Creativity Lab is an independent initiative with finite time, technical capacity, and financial resources.

The founder and maintainer determines which projects receive active attention, taking into account:

- Alignment with the Lab's mission.
- Research, technical, and creative value.
- Feasibility and available expertise.
- Resource requirements and opportunity cost.
- External dependencies and continuity risks.
- Security, privacy, and legal exposure.
- Expected maintenance and support obligations.
- Long-term relevance and potential for learning.

Acceptance of a proposal for exploration does not commit the Lab to complete, publish, commercialize, or maintain it.

A project may be paused or discontinued when priorities, resources, assumptions, or risk conditions change. Where the change materially affects a public representation, the project record and relevant release information should be updated.

The Lab may maintain records of material governance decisions to support accountability and continuity.

Records may include:

- Policy versions and revision history.
- Project acceptance, pause, or discontinuation decisions.
- Delegation of responsibilities and authority.
- Material architecture or scope decisions.
- Publication approvals and withdrawals.
- Significant collaboration arrangements.
- Repository access decisions.
- Material security, privacy, or confidentiality decisions.
- Changes to the governance structure.

A proportionate record should identify the date, decision, responsible authority, rationale, and relevant conditions or follow-up actions.

Public records should communicate relevant decisions without exposing credentials, personal data, confidential agreements, exploitable security details, or other restricted information. Sensitive records must be retained in an appropriately restricted location.

## 17. Continuity and Succession

The Lab's continuity depends on clear documentation, responsible access management, and preservation of important project and policy records.

Where practicable, the maintainer should ensure that the Lab retains:

- Version-controlled policies and governance records.
- Clear repository and project documentation.
- Recoverable project files and appropriate backups.
- Documented dependencies and operating requirements.
- Securely managed access and recovery arrangements.
- Records of material commitments and rights.

If the maintainer becomes unavailable, project activity may be paused until an authorized successor or alternative arrangement is established.

No contributor, collaborator, or repository participant automatically becomes the successor, owner, or administrator of the Lab through contribution history or repository activity.

Any transfer of governance authority, administrative control, or ownership must be explicitly authorized and handled in accordance with applicable rights and agreements.

## 18. Governance Changes and Delegation

The governance model may evolve in response to actual operational needs, changes in scale, or new forms of participation.

Possible future arrangements may include delegated project maintainers, technical leads, external advisors, or project-specific review arrangements. These possibilities do not constitute current appointments or organizational commitments.

### 18.1 Delegation

A delegation must define, as appropriate:

- The person or role receiving the delegation.
- The responsibility and scope of authority.
- Decisions that remain reserved to the maintainer.
- Relevant access permissions.
- Reporting or documentation expectations.
- Duration, review, and withdrawal conditions.

Delegated authority must not be assumed from participation, seniority, technical expertise, or repository permissions alone.

### 18.2 Changes to Structure or Authority

Changes that materially alter decision rights, accountability, or the organizational structure require maintainer approval and must be documented.

The Lab should introduce formal roles only when they have a real purpose, clear responsibilities, and an accurately described level of authority.

### 18.3 Policy Amendments

The founder and maintainer is responsible for reviewing this document when the governance model, Lab activities, applicable requirements, or related policies materially change.

- Whether delegated responsibilities are documented.
- Whether project fields remain aligned with their authoritative policies.

## 21. Closing Principle

Governance exists to provide direction, clarity, accountability, and continuity.

It should protect the conditions that allow independent research and creative experimentation to flourish while ensuring that responsibility remains visible and decisions remain understandable.

A flexible structure does not mean an absence of rules. An independent initiative still requires clear boundaries, accountable decisions, and respect for the people, rights, and work involved.

**Independence requires responsibility. Creativity requires freedom. Good governance makes room for both.**
