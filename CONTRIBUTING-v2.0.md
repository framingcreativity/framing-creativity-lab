## 1. Purpose

This document defines the principles, requirements, and procedures governing contributions and collaboration within Framing Creativity Lab (the Lab).

It establishes a consistent framework for individuals who wish to propose research, report issues, contribute technical or creative work, improve documentation, or participate in an authorised collaboration.

Its objectives are to:

- Explain how contributions may be proposed and submitted.
- Define contributor responsibilities.
- Establish the review and acceptance process.
- Protect intellectual property and publication rights.
- Preserve confidentiality, privacy, and security.
- Maintain technical and research integrity.
- Clarify the authority and responsibilities of the maintainer.
- Ensure that contributions remain consistent with the Lab's principles and policies.

These guidelines apply to contributions made to official public repositories and projects operated under the Framing Creativity Lab identity.

**A contribution is an opportunity to improve the work, not an automatic entitlement to participate in its governance, ownership, or publication.**

## 2. Scope and Authority

### 2.1 Scope

These guidelines apply to contributions and proposals involving:

- Research and technical investigations.
- Software and application development.
- Artificial intelligence and machine learning.
- Creative technology and computational design.
- Prototypes and proofs of concept.
- Developer tools and reusable components.
- Technical documentation and research publications.
- Interface, accessibility, and design improvements.
- Other activities explicitly associated with the Lab.

The requirements apply regardless of whether a contribution is submitted through an issue, pull request, direct collaboration, or another authorised process.

This document defines the practical contribution process. It does not replace the Lab's governing documents.

- Contributions are automatically eligible for publication.

Contribution opportunities and access permissions are determined according to the actual needs and governance of each project.

## 3. Contribution Philosophy

Framing Creativity Lab values contributions that support meaningful research, responsible experimentation, technical quality, and creative exploration.

Contributions should be constructive, documented, and proportionate to their intended purpose.

### 3.1 Core Principles

**Quality over quantity**

A focused, well-supported contribution is more valuable than a large submission that cannot be adequately reviewed or maintained.

**Clarity over complexity**

Contributions should address identifiable objectives without introducing unnecessary technical or organisational complexity.

**Evidence over assertion**

Technical and research claims should be supported by relevant evidence, documented reasoning, or clearly identified assumptions.

**Transparency**

Relevant methods, dependencies, limitations, sources, and external assistance should be disclosed.

**Respect for intellectual property**

Contributors must have the necessary rights and permissions for every material they submit.

**Confidentiality by design**

Private, proprietary, restricted, or confidential information must not be introduced into public repositories without an appropriate and explicitly authorised basis.

**Constructive collaboration**

Disagreement is welcome when it is handled through evidence, reasoned discussion, and professional conduct.

### 3.2 Contributor Background

Contributions may come from individuals with different professional and academic backgrounds.

Relevant experience may include:

- Software engineering.
- Applied AI and machine learning.
- Research and experimentation.
- Creative coding and digital art.
- UX and interface design.
- Digital product development.
- Technical writing.
- Data and systems engineering.
- Other disciplines relevant to a specific project.

Formal qualifications are not required for every contribution.

However, contributors must be able to explain the work they submit and provide sufficient context for meaningful evaluation.

## 4. Governance and Contribution Authority

### 4.1 Current Governance Model

Framing Creativity Lab currently operates as an independent, founder-led initiative.

J-ART is the founder and maintainer and retains responsibility for:

- The Lab's direction and priorities.
- Repository administration.
- Project selection and acceptance.
- Contribution review and integration.
- Technical and editorial standards.
- Governance and policy maintenance.
- Intellectual property and confidentiality controls.
- Publication decisions.
- Significant collaboration arrangements.

This reflects the Lab's current operating structure. It does not imply the existence of a formal board, external governing committee, or independent internal appeal body.

### 4.2 Maintainer Authority

The maintainer may:

- Approve a contribution for integration.
- Request modifications or clarification.
- Require additional testing or documentation.
- Request evidence of ownership or permission.
- Defer a submission for further assessment.
- Reject or close a contribution.
- Restrict or remove access where appropriate.
- Decline publication independently of technical acceptance.

These decisions must respect applicable law, contractual obligations, licences, and third-party rights.

### 4.3 Contributor Responsibilities

Contributors are responsible for:

- The legitimacy of the material they submit.
- The accuracy of their statements and technical claims.
- Compliance with applicable licences and terms.
- Disclosure of relevant third-party dependencies.
- Protecting confidential and personal information.
- Providing adequate documentation.
- Responding to reasonable review questions.
- Respecting the Lab's governance and behavioural expectations.

### 4.4 Repository Permissions

Participation does not automatically grant repository permissions.

Access is separately authorised and should follow the principle of least privilege.

The maintainer determines whether a contributor requires read, triage, write, maintain, or administrative access.

Administrative authority remains reserved to the maintainer unless explicitly delegated.

## 6. Before Contributing

Before starting substantial work, contributors should understand the Lab's purpose, boundaries, and operating principles.

### 6.1 Review the Existing Framework

Contributors should consult the relevant governing documents and project documentation.

At minimum, review:

- The Lab's identity and operating boundaries.
- The project's purpose and scope.
- The relevant technical and research requirements.
- The publication and intellectual property conditions.
- The security and behavioural requirements.

### 6.2 Check Existing Work

Before proposing a new feature or experiment:

- Search for related projects and existing functionality.
- Review relevant issues and discussions.
- Examine the current implementation.
- Identify existing dependencies and constraints.
- Check whether similar work is already underway.
- Consider whether the proposal fits the project's scope.

This helps avoid duplicated effort and unnecessary complexity.

### 6.3 Discuss Substantial Changes

Substantial changes should be discussed before implementation where practical.

Examples include:

- New project directions.
- Major architectural changes.
- New external services.
- Significant dependencies.
- Changes affecting data handling.
- Changes affecting security or privacy.
- Major API modifications.
- Changes involving intellectual property or publication rights.

A discussion or proposal does not constitute approval to proceed.

## 7. Proposing a Project or Experiment

New project proposals should provide sufficient information for an initial assessment.

### 7.1 Proposal Information

A proposal should include the following where relevant.

**Project identity**

- Proposed name.
- Research domain.
- Project type.
- Short description.

**Research context**

- Problem or question.
- Motivation.
- Existing approaches.
- Expected contribution.

**Objectives**

- Primary objective.
- Secondary objectives.
- Intended outcome.
- Success criteria.

**Technical approach**

- Proposed methodology.
- Technologies and frameworks.
- Relevant dependencies.
- Known constraints.

**Evaluation**

- Expected results.
- Evaluation criteria.
- Testing approach.
- Known limitations.

**Publication considerations**

- Ownership and authorship.
- Third-party resources.
- Data requirements.
- Security and privacy considerations.
- Intended publication scope.

### 7.2 Proposal Assessment

The maintainer may assess a proposal according to:

- Alignment with the Lab's mission.
- Research or practical value.
- Technical feasibility.
- Originality or relevance.
- Resource requirements.
- Existing alternatives.
- Risk and maintenance implications.
- Publication eligibility.

A proposal may be accepted for exploration, revised, deferred, or declined.

Acceptance of a proposal does not guarantee implementation, completion, publication, commercialisation, or ongoing maintenance.

## 8. Issues and Reporting

Issues should contain sufficient information to support investigation.

### 8.1 Bug Reports

A useful bug report should include:

- A concise title.
- A clear description.
- Relevant environment information.
- Steps to reproduce.
- Expected behaviour.
- Actual behaviour.
- Error messages or logs where appropriate.
- Relevant screenshots or examples.
- Possible workarounds, if known.

### 8.2 Feature Requests

Feature requests should explain:

- The problem being addressed.
- Why the functionality may be useful.
- Expected behaviour.
- Relevant use cases.
- Potential technical considerations.

Requests should focus on the problem rather than impose an unnecessarily restrictive implementation.

### 8.3 Research Discussions

Research-related issues should identify:

- The question being investigated.
- Relevant context and references.
- Proposed methodology.
- Expected research value.
- Known uncertainties.

### 8.4 Sensitive Information

Do not include the following in public issues:

- Credentials or access tokens.
- Personal or sensitive information.
- Confidential project material.
- Private infrastructure details.
- Proprietary source code.
- Restricted datasets.
- Security vulnerabilities.

Security vulnerabilities must be reported through the private procedure described in `SECURITY-v2.0.md`.

Do not assume that a private reporting feature is available unless the repository explicitly provides it.

### 8.5 Discussion Quality

Avoid:

- Duplicate reports.
- Unsupported accusations.
- Unnecessary personal information.
- Credentials or private data.
- Personal attacks.
- Unrelated discussions.
- Requests that clearly fall outside the project's scope.

## 9. Development Workflow

Contributions involving code should follow a structured and reviewable process.

### 9.1 Repository Preparation

Before modifying code:

1. Review the project documentation.
2. Inspect the repository structure.
3. Understand the relevant architecture.
4. Identify existing implementation conventions.
5. Confirm the objective being addressed.
6. Establish the expected scope of the change.
7. Verify that the work is authorised for the target repository.

### 9.2 Forking and Branching

Where external contributions are accepted, contributors should generally work through a fork or an explicitly authorised branch.

Recommended branch prefixes:

| Prefix | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `feature/` | New functionality |
| `fix/` | Bug correction |
| `docs/` | Documentation |
| `refactor/` | Code restructuring |
| `test/` | Testing improvements |
| `research/` | Research experiments |
| `prototype/` | Prototype development |
| `chore/` | Maintenance tasks |

Examples:

- `feature/structured-ai-output`
- `fix/input-validation`
- `docs/installation-guide`
- `research/model-comparison`

Branch names should be descriptive and concise.

### 9.3 Focused Changes

Each contribution should have a clear objective.

Avoid combining unrelated changes in a single submission.

Substantial work should be divided into smaller, independently reviewable steps where practical.

### 9.4 Local Validation

Before submitting changes:

- Verify the implementation.
- Run relevant tests.
- Check formatting and linting where configured.
- Review the final diff.
- Inspect generated files.
- Check for accidental configuration changes.
- Confirm that documentation remains accurate.
- Remove secrets and unintended personal or confidential data.

Contributors must not claim that validation or tests have passed unless they were actually performed.

### 9.5 Pull Requests

A pull request should explain:

- What has changed.
- Why the change was made.
- Which issue or objective it addresses.
- How it was implemented.
- Which tests or checks were performed.
- Any known limitations.
- Any relevant screenshots or demonstrations.
- Relevant dependencies and third-party material.
- Any material ownership or permission considerations.

Contributors should keep pull requests focused and provide sufficient context for meaningful review.

Submitting a pull request does not guarantee review within a particular timeframe or acceptance.

## 10. Technical Quality Standards

Code contributions should reflect the technical maturity and conventions of the destination project.

### 10.1 General Principles

- Prefer readable and maintainable implementations.
- Use descriptive names.
- Separate responsibilities clearly.
- Avoid unnecessary abstractions.
- Minimise duplicated logic.
- Validate external inputs.
- Handle errors appropriately.
- Avoid undocumented behaviour.
- Respect existing architecture unless a change is justified.
- Consider accessibility, security, and maintainability where relevant.

### 10.2 Dependencies

New dependencies should be justified.

Before introducing a dependency, consider:

- Whether equivalent functionality already exists.
- Maintenance activity.
- Security history.
- Licence compatibility.
- Runtime or bundle impact.
- Dependency complexity.
- Long-term availability.

Third-party dependencies must be identified and reviewed under the applicable project and publication requirements.

### 10.3 Configuration

Configuration should be explicit and documented.

Sensitive information must not be hardcoded or committed.

Provide safe example configuration where appropriate.

### 10.4 Testing

Testing should be proportionate to the nature and risk of the change.

Examples include:

- Unit tests.
- Integration tests.
- Functional tests.
- Regression tests.
- End-to-end tests.
- Performance tests.
- Accessibility checks.
- Security checks.

The scope and limitations of testing should be reported accurately.

### 10.5 AI-Assisted Development

AI-assisted development is permitted where appropriate.

Contributors remain responsible for the material they submit.

They must:

- Review generated implementations.
- Check dependencies and licences.
- Consider security implications.
- Verify relevant technical claims.
- Test generated code.
- Disclose material AI assistance where relevant to authorship, provenance, or reproducibility.

Private prompts, proprietary workflows, confidential configurations, and restricted project information must not be submitted.

AI assistance does not replace contributor responsibility or independent review.

## 11. Research Contribution Standards

Research contributions must follow the applicable requirements of `RESEARCH-METHODOLOGY-v2.0.md`.

### 11.1 Research Integrity

Contributors must not:

- Fabricate results.
- Alter observations to support a hypothesis.
- Conceal relevant failures.
- Misrepresent experimental conditions.
- Present assumptions as established findings.
- Misattribute sources.
- Claim independent verification without appropriate evaluation.
- Exaggerate the significance or reliability of results.

### 11.2 Source Attribution

Research sources must be identified clearly.

Where applicable, include:

- Author.
- Title.
- Publication date.
- Source URL.
- Relevant page or section.
- Access date.

Direct quotations and third-party materials must respect applicable rights and attribution requirements.

### 11.3 Experimental Records

Substantial research contributions should document:

- Research objective.
- Methodology.
- Technical environment.
- Experimental conditions.
- Inputs and outputs.
- Results.
- Evaluation.
- Limitations.
- Conclusions.

### 11.4 AI Research

AI-related contributions should identify relevant information where applicable:

- Models and providers.
- Model versions.
- Configuration and parameters.
- Evaluation conditions.
- Test cases.
- Human interventions.
- Relevant prompts or methodological details.
- Known limitations.

Sensitive or proprietary prompts and configurations must not be disclosed.

## 12. Documentation Standards

Documentation is an integral part of a contribution.

### 12.1 Documentation Principles

Documentation should be:

- Clear.
- Accurate.
- Structured.
- Consistent.
- Relevant.
- Accessible.
- Traceable where appropriate.
- Maintained when necessary.

### 12.2 Documentation Changes

When code changes affect usage or behaviour, the corresponding documentation should be updated.

This may include:

- Installation procedures.
- Configuration.
- API behaviour.
- Command-line usage.
- Dependencies.
- Screenshots.
- Known limitations.
- Architecture.
- Testing procedures.

### 12.3 Language

English is the primary language for public technical documentation.

Other languages may be included where they add value, provided terminology and meaning remain consistent.

### 12.4 Claims and Maturity

Documentation must accurately describe the project's maturity and operating conditions.

Do not describe an experimental prototype as production-ready or a limited test as comprehensive validation.

Technical maturity, operational readiness, project status, and publication status must remain distinct.

## 13. Intellectual Property, Ownership, and Licensing

### 13.1 Ownership of Contributions

Contributors retain whatever rights they hold in their original contributions, subject to applicable law and any explicit agreements.

Submitting a contribution does not automatically transfer ownership to Framing Creativity Lab.

The Lab does not claim ownership of a contribution solely because it has been proposed, reviewed, accepted, or included in a repository.

### 13.2 Permission to Submit

Contributors must have the right to submit the material they provide.

They must not submit:

- Employer-owned code without authorisation.
- Client-owned material without permission.
- Confidential commercial implementations.
- Unauthorised third-party assets.
- Restricted datasets.
- Material subject to incompatible licensing conditions.
- Private or proprietary material from unrelated projects.

Where ownership or permission is uncertain, the material must not be submitted for public incorporation until the issue is resolved.

### 13.3 Permission for Incorporation and Distribution

Submission, review, acceptance, or inclusion in a repository does not independently grant the Lab unrestricted permission to:

- Reproduce the contribution.
- Modify or create derivative works.
- Distribute or publish the contribution.
- Sublicense the contribution.
- Commercially exploit the contribution.

The permissions required for the intended incorporation, publication, and distribution must be established through an applicable licence or explicit written agreement.

The rights granted must be sufficiently clear in scope and must not be assumed from silence, repository access, participation, or the act of submitting a pull request.

Where the necessary permissions are not established, the contribution must not be merged or published as Lab material.

### 13.4 No General Open-Source Licence

The public Framing Creativity Lab repository currently has no general open-source licence.

Public availability does not, by itself, grant permission to reproduce, modify, distribute, sublicense, or commercially exploit original material.

A project-specific licence or rights statement must be explicit and must identify the material it covers.

These contribution guidelines do not create a general contribution licence or override the rights applicable to individual projects.

### 13.5 Third-Party Material

External code, assets, datasets, documentation, models, fonts, media, and other resources must be identified.

Contributors must consider:

- Ownership.
- Applicable licences.
- Attribution requirements.
- Redistribution permissions.
- Usage restrictions.
- Provider terms.
- Relevant contractual obligations.

Required notices and attribution must be preserved.

Material with unclear ownership or licensing conditions must not be distributed until the relevant rights have been clarified.

### 13.6 AI-Generated Material

Contributors must consider applicable model, provider, and service terms when submitting AI-generated material.

They should disclose material AI assistance where relevant to authorship, provenance, evaluation, or reproducibility.

AI-generated content must not automatically be assumed to be free of third-party rights or restrictions.

### 13.7 Ownership Uncertainty

Where ownership, permission, licensing compatibility, or attribution cannot be established, the contribution must be held from incorporation and publication until the issue is resolved.

An assumption that material is free to use because it is publicly accessible is not sufficient evidence of permission.

## 14. Confidentiality, Privacy, and Security

The protection of sensitive information is mandatory.

### 14.1 Prohibited Submissions

Contributors must not submit:

- Passwords.
- API keys.
- Access tokens.
- Private certificates.
- Credentials.
- Confidential client data.
- Proprietary commercial code.
- Private product architecture.
- Internal operational documentation.
- Unauthorised personal information.
- Restricted datasets.
- Private infrastructure configuration.
- Confidential prompts or workflows.
- Material from private projects without authorisation.

### 14.2 Public Repository Boundary

The public Lab repository is an independent research environment.

It must not be used as a storage, mirror, or distribution channel for private, proprietary, or confidential projects.

A related idea, general technique, or research interest does not authorise publication of private implementation details, source code, workflows, prompts, datasets, credentials, or internal documentation.

Changing a name, removing a brand, or extracting a small fragment does not automatically make protected material suitable for publication.

### 14.3 Data Handling

Use synthetic, public, appropriately licensed, or otherwise authorised data wherever practical.

Personal, sensitive, confidential, or restricted information must not be included without an appropriate legal basis, authorisation, and safeguards.

Applicable data classification and handling requirements are defined by `PUBLICATION-POLICY-v2.0.md` and `RESPONSIBLE-AI-v2.0.md`.

### 14.4 Security Vulnerabilities

Security vulnerabilities must be reported privately through the process described in `SECURITY-v2.0.md`.

Do not publish exploitable vulnerability details in public issues, pull requests, or discussions.

### 14.5 Review and Removal

The maintainer may reject, restrict, or remove submissions that contain unauthorised, confidential, sensitive, or otherwise unsuitable material.

Where appropriate, additional information may be requested before a decision is made.

## 15. Review and Acceptance

All contributions are subject to review before integration.

### 15.1 Review Dimensions

The review may consider the following areas.

**Relevance**
- Does the contribution align with the project?
- Does it address an identifiable objective?

**Technical quality**
- Is the implementation appropriate?
- Is it understandable and maintainable?
- Are dependencies justified?

**Research integrity**
- Are claims supported?
- Are methods and limitations documented?

**Security and privacy**
- Are sensitive values or unsafe behaviours present?
- Are data and external inputs handled appropriately?

**Ownership and permissions**
- Are authorship and ownership sufficiently clear?
- Are the permissions required for incorporation and distribution established?
- Are third-party terms compatible?

**Documentation**
- Is the contribution sufficiently explained?
- Are relevant project records updated?

**Maintenance**
- Can the implementation reasonably be maintained?
- Are its ongoing requirements understood?

### 15.2 Review Outcomes

A contribution may receive one of the following outcomes:

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `Approved` | The contribution has been accepted for integration, subject to any outstanding conditions being satisfied. |
| `Changes Requested` | Modifications or additional information are required. |
| `Under Review` | Further assessment is necessary. |
| `Deferred` | The contribution may be reconsidered later. |
| `Rejected` | The contribution will not be integrated. |
| `Closed` | The discussion or submission has ended. |

An approval for technical integration does not independently constitute publication approval.

### 15.3 Changes Requested

When modifications are requested, contributors should respond to review comments and update their submissions.

Unresolved issues may prevent acceptance.

### 15.4 Rejection

A contribution may be declined for technical, research, security, privacy, ownership, publication, governance, or scope-related reasons.

A technically functional contribution may still be unsuitable for integration.

Where appropriate, the reason for rejection should be communicated clearly.

### 15.5 No Guaranteed Acceptance or Publication

The maintainer is not obligated to accept, integrate, maintain, or publish a contribution.

A successful review, merge, or technical demonstration does not independently authorise public release.

Publication remains subject to `PUBLICATION-POLICY-v2.0.md`.

## 16. Communication and Collaboration

### 16.1 Communication Principles

Participants should communicate professionally and respectfully.

Technical disagreement is acceptable and may improve the quality of research and implementation.

Harassment, discriminatory conduct, personal attacks, and deliberately disruptive behaviour are not acceptable.

### 16.2 Discussion Quality

Contributors should:

- Explain technical decisions.
- Provide relevant evidence.
- Ask focused questions.
- Distinguish facts from opinions.
- Acknowledge uncertainty.
- Respect different areas of expertise.
- Avoid unnecessary confrontation.

### 16.3 Responsiveness

Contributors should make reasonable efforts to respond to review questions and requests for clarification.

There is no guarantee of immediate review, response, or integration.

### 16.4 Collaboration Boundaries

Participation in a project does not automatically create:

- Employment.
- A partnership.
- A joint venture.
- Joint ownership.
- An agency relationship.
- A formal organisational affiliation.

Substantive collaborations involving confidential information, intellectual property, commercial rights, financial commitments, or joint ownership should be documented through an explicit agreement before work begins.

### 16.5 External Representation

Only individuals explicitly authorised by the founder and maintainer may represent the Lab officially or make commitments on its behalf.

Contributors must not announce partnerships, endorsements, or organisational commitments without authorisation.

## 17. Contributor Recognition and Attribution

Meaningful contributions should be recognised accurately.

Recognition may include:

- Git commit attribution.
- Pull request history.
- Contributor listings.
- Project documentation.
- Acknowledgements.
- Research credits.

Attribution should reflect the actual contribution.

Submitting a minor correction does not automatically establish authorship of an entire project.

Similarly, substantial contributions by others must not be misrepresented as the sole work of the maintainer.

Where authorship, attribution, or recognition is unclear, it should be discussed before publication.

Attribution does not replace ownership verification or publication permission.

## 18. Project-Specific Contribution Requirements

Individual projects may establish additional contribution requirements.

These may include:

- Coding conventions.
- Required test coverage.
- Architecture constraints.
- Research documentation formats.
- Data-handling procedures.
- Security checks.
- Design and accessibility standards.
- Specific licensing conditions.
- Additional review requirements.

Project-specific requirements may introduce stricter conditions than these guidelines.

They must not be used to bypass the Lab's fundamental governance, confidentiality, ownership, security, or publication requirements.

Where a project has no additional contribution documentation, these guidelines apply by default.

This document should be reviewed when:

- Contribution procedures materially change.

### 21.3 Versioning

Substantive revisions should update the document version and be recorded in version control.

The version history should distinguish:

- **Major revisions:** Fundamental changes to contribution rights, governance, or core requirements.
- **Minor revisions:** New procedures or substantive additions.
- **Patch revisions:** Editorial corrections and clarifications.

Changes to these guidelines must be checked against the Lab Charter, Governance, Publication Policy, Security Policy, and Code of Conduct.

Where a conflict is identified, the relevant governing or specialist policy must be considered before these guidelines are revised.
