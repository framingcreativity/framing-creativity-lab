## 1. Purpose

Framing Creativity Lab (the Lab) aims to provide a constructive, respectful, and intellectually open environment for research, experimentation, development, and creative collaboration.

The Lab brings together different disciplines, technical backgrounds, creative practices, and perspectives. Meaningful collaboration depends not only on technical standards, but also on the way people communicate, exchange ideas, challenge assumptions, and work through disagreements.

This Code of Conduct establishes the behavioural expectations that apply to everyone interacting with the Lab.

Its objectives are to:

- Encourage respectful and constructive collaboration.
- Maintain an environment open to different perspectives and disciplines.
- Support rigorous discussion without personal hostility.
- Establish clear boundaries for acceptable behaviour.
- Define a proportionate approach to reporting and addressing misconduct.
- Protect contributors, participants, maintainers, and collaborators from harassment, intimidation, discrimination, and abuse.
- Clarify responsibilities and limitations in the handling of conduct-related concerns.
- Preserve the integrity of the Lab's research and collaborative activities.

This Code applies regardless of professional status, experience, technical expertise, contribution history, or role within the Lab.

**Intellectual freedom requires mutual respect, clear boundaries, and accountability.**

---

## 2. Scope

This Code applies to all spaces and activities officially associated with Framing Creativity Lab, including:

- Public GitHub repositories.
- Issues and pull requests.
- Code reviews and technical discussions.
- Research proposals and experiment submissions.
- Documentation and collaborative writing.
- Project demonstrations and public presentations.
- Official communication channels.
- Lab-related online meetings or events.
- Collaborative activities conducted under the Lab's identity.
- Other spaces where an individual is formally representing the Lab.

The Code applies whether an interaction takes place publicly or privately, online or in person, provided that it is connected to Lab activities.

It also applies to maintainers, contributors, and collaborators when they represent the Lab or exercise authority within its projects.

Conduct outside these spaces may fall within the scope of this Code where it has a material and credible connection to participation, safety, or legitimate activities of the Lab.

## 3. Guiding Principles

The Lab is built around the following principles.

### 3.1 Respect

Treat people with dignity, regardless of their background, experience, identity, professional position, or personal circumstances.

Disagreement is acceptable. Disrespect is not.

### 3.2 Intellectual Openness

Encourage the exchange of ideas, critical thinking, experimentation, and constructive questioning.

No contributor should be expected to agree with every proposal, interpretation, or decision.

### 3.3 Constructive Criticism

Challenge ideas, methods, assumptions, and technical decisions without attacking the individuals presenting them.

Criticism should be relevant, proportionate, and supported by reasoning whenever possible.

### 3.4 Curiosity and Learning

Recognize that the Lab is a space for exploration.

Mistakes, incomplete experiments, unsuccessful prototypes, and changing perspectives are part of the research and development process.

### 3.5 Accountability

Everyone is responsible for their conduct and its impact on others.

When mistakes occur, participants are expected to acknowledge them, engage constructively, and make reasonable efforts to correct their behaviour.

### 3.6 Transparency

Communicate expectations, decisions, and project limitations clearly.

Do not use ambiguity, hidden criteria, or personal influence to undermine fair collaboration.

Transparency does not override privacy, confidentiality, security, or intellectual property rights.

### 3.7 Fair Treatment

Concerns should be considered according to their substance, context, and available evidence.

Personal disagreements, criticism, or rejected proposals must not automatically be treated as misconduct.

## 4. Expected Behaviour

All participants are expected to:

- Communicate respectfully and professionally.
- Assume good faith while remaining open to evidence and clarification.
- Listen to different perspectives.
- Provide constructive and relevant feedback.
- Respect the time and contributions of others.
- Acknowledge sources, authorship, and intellectual contributions.
- Respect intellectual property and licensing requirements.
- Recognize the limits of their own expertise.
- Accept reasonable criticism and review.
- Disagree without becoming hostile or personal.
- Respect project scope, technical decisions, and established contribution processes.
- Protect private, confidential, and sensitive information.
- Raise concerns through appropriate channels.
- Respect reasonable communication boundaries.
- Contribute to an environment where people can participate without intimidation or harassment.

Participants should also be mindful of different communication styles, levels of technical knowledge, cultural contexts, and language abilities.

Clarity is more valuable than performative expertise.

## 5. Unacceptable Behaviour

The following behaviours are not acceptable within Lab spaces or activities.

### 5.1 Harassment and Discrimination

Examples include:

- Personal attacks, insults, or degrading remarks.
- Harassment based on identity or personal characteristics.
- Sexist, racist, homophobic, transphobic, or otherwise discriminatory conduct.
- Unwelcome sexual attention or inappropriate personal remarks.
- Deliberate humiliation or intimidation.
- Repeated unwanted contact after a request to stop.
- Targeted behaviour intended to exclude or undermine an individual.

### 5.2 Abuse of Authority

Examples include:

- Using administrative or technical privileges to intimidate others.
- Threatening contributors for expressing disagreement.
- Unreasonably obstructing participation through personal hostility.
- Using influence within the Lab to retaliate against criticism.
- Misrepresenting decisions or authority to pressure participants.
- Using access permissions or repository control for an improper personal purpose.
- Applying rules selectively to punish legitimate disagreement.

### 5.3 Disruptive Conduct

Examples include:

- Repeatedly derailing discussions through hostile or irrelevant behaviour.
- Deliberately provoking or escalating conflict.
- Publishing private disputes to intimidate or embarrass individuals.
- Repeatedly ignoring reasonable communication boundaries.
- Flooding discussions with abusive, misleading, or disruptive content.
- Deliberately interfering with legitimate project or research activities.

### 5.4 Misrepresentation and Intellectual Misconduct

Examples include:

- Presenting another person's work as one's own.
- Knowingly misrepresenting another contributor's statements or work.
- Deliberately concealing material conflicts of interest.
- Falsifying research findings, test results, or technical evidence.
- Claiming authorship or ownership without a legitimate basis.
- Using contributions outside their authorized scope.
- Knowingly misrepresenting the extent of another person's contribution.
- Deliberately manipulating records or evidence relevant to a review.

### 5.5 Privacy and Confidentiality Violations

Examples include:

- Publishing personal information without authorization.
- Disclosing private conversations without an appropriate basis.
- Exposing credentials, private project material, or confidential research.
- Sharing private files, screenshots, or identifying information to intimidate or harm someone.
- Accessing or distributing information beyond one's authorized permissions.
- Using confidential information for retaliation or personal advantage.

### 5.6 Retaliation

Retaliation against someone who reports misconduct, participates in a review, provides relevant information, or raises a concern in good faith is prohibited.

This includes:

- Threats or intimidation.
- Harassment or unwanted contact.
- Deliberate exclusion from legitimate participation.
- Misuse of access permissions.
- Unjustified interference with contributions or project activities.
- Attempts to punish or discredit someone for raising a concern.

A report that cannot be substantiated is not automatically malicious or dishonest.

However, knowingly fabricated reports or deliberately manipulated evidence may themselves constitute misconduct.

## 6. Differences of Opinion and Technical Disagreement

The Lab encourages rigorous debate.

Technical decisions, research interpretations, creative directions, and methodological choices may be challenged.

Participants are encouraged to:

- Explain their reasoning.
- Provide relevant evidence.
- Distinguish facts from assumptions.
- Identify trade-offs and limitations.
- Consider alternative approaches.
- Acknowledge uncertainty.
- Accept that a discussion may end without complete agreement.

The following distinction is fundamental:

| Constructive disagreement | Unacceptable conduct |
|---|---|
| Challenging an architectural decision | Attacking the person who proposed it |
| Questioning research methodology | Dismissing someone through humiliation |
| Requesting evidence | Using intimidation to silence discussion |
| Disagreeing with a creative direction | Discrediting someone's identity or ability |
| Rejecting a proposal with clear reasoning | Retaliating against its author |
| Criticising a decision or policy | Harassing or threatening the decision-maker |
| Raising a governance concern | Deliberately publishing private information to intimidate someone |

Not every disagreement is a conflict, and not every rejected proposal represents unfair treatment.

The maintainer retains responsibility for project decisions under the current governance model. That authority does not exempt the maintainer from the expectations defined in this Code.

Legitimate criticism of the Lab, its policies, or its decisions must not be treated as misconduct solely because it is critical or unwelcome.

## 7. Maintainer and Participant Responsibilities

### 7.1 Maintainer Responsibilities

The founder and maintainer is responsible for applying this Code within the Lab's spaces and activities.

This includes:

- Communicating behavioural expectations.
- Reviewing reported concerns in good faith.
- Taking reasonable action when misconduct is identified.
- Protecting reporters and affected individuals from retaliation.
- Applying measures proportionately.
- Avoiding unnecessary disclosure of sensitive information.
- Considering conflicts of interest.
- Documenting significant enforcement decisions where appropriate.
- Reviewing this Code when the Lab's structure or activities evolve.

### 7.2 Responsibilities of Contributors and Collaborators

Contributors and collaborators are expected to:

- Follow this Code.
- Respect the boundaries of their assigned responsibilities.
- Communicate professionally.
- Raise relevant concerns through appropriate channels.
- Cooperate reasonably with conduct-related reviews.
- Avoid retaliation against participants who raise concerns.
- Protect confidential and personal information.
- Respect decisions made within the Lab's legitimate governance authority.

### 7.3 Governance Limitations

The Lab currently operates under a founder-led governance model.

There is no formally established moderation committee, independent ombudsperson, or dedicated disciplinary body.

The founder and maintainer retains final internal decision-making authority, subject to applicable law, contracts, platform procedures, and third-party rights.

This structure must be represented accurately. The Lab must not imply the existence of independent adjudication or appeal arrangements that have not been established.

## 8. Reporting a Concern

Anyone who experiences or witnesses conduct that may violate this Code is encouraged to report it.

Reports may concern:

- Harassment or discrimination.
- Threatening or abusive behaviour.
- Retaliation.
- Misuse of authority.
- Privacy or confidentiality violations.
- Repeated disruptive conduct.
- Misrepresentation or intellectual misconduct.
- Other behaviour that materially affects the safety or integrity of Lab activities.

### 8.1 Reporting Channel

Reports should be submitted privately through a reporting method explicitly published by Framing Creativity Lab.

A public GitHub issue, pull request, or discussion thread should not be used for sensitive allegations or personal information.

**Operational status:** An accessible private conduct-reporting channel has not yet been verified as published for the Lab. The reporting process must not be described as fully operational until such a channel has been established and communicated.

No unpublished email address, personal contact method, or private communication channel should be assumed.

### 8.2 Reports Involving the Maintainer

Where a concern involves the founder or maintainer directly, the reporting process requires particular care.

The Lab does not currently have an independent internal complaints body.

A person raising such a concern should not be led to believe that the maintainer's own assessment constitutes independent review.

Where appropriate, relevant external platform reporting mechanisms, contractual channels, or competent authorities may be used.

Any external reviewer or alternative process must be explicitly established; it must not be represented as an existing Lab institution unless it has actually been appointed.

### 8.3 Information to Include

A report should include, where possible:

- A description of the incident.
- The relevant date or approximate timeframe.
- The space or project where it occurred.
- The individuals involved, where known.
- Relevant messages, links, screenshots, or other evidence.
- Any previous attempts to resolve the situation.
- The impact of the behaviour.
- Any immediate concerns about safety, privacy, or retaliation.
- Any protective measures that may be needed.

Reports should be factual and proportionate.

A reporter does not need to provide every detail immediately, especially where doing so could expose them to further harm.

### 8.4 Confidentiality

Reports should be handled with discretion.

Information should be shared only to the extent reasonably necessary to assess and address the concern.

However, absolute confidentiality and anonymity cannot be guaranteed.

Some information may need to be disclosed to understand the incident, provide a fair opportunity to respond, protect affected individuals, or comply with applicable legal obligations.

The identity of a reporter should not be disclosed unnecessarily.

### 8.5 Immediate Safety Concerns

Where a concern involves an immediate threat to personal safety, credible threats of violence, or other urgent circumstances, individuals should use appropriate emergency or competent external services.

The Lab is not an emergency response service and cannot guarantee immediate intervention.

## 9. Review and Assessment Process

Reports should be assessed through a proportionate and reasonable process.

### Stage 1 — Initial Review

The responsible reviewer considers the nature, scope, urgency, and potential impact of the report.

Additional information may be requested where necessary.

Where immediate protective measures are reasonably necessary, they may be considered before the full assessment is complete.

### Stage 2 — Assessment

The review should consider:

- The reported behaviour.
- Available evidence.
- The context of the interaction.
- The impact on affected individuals.
- Whether the behaviour appears intentional, repeated, or particularly serious.
- Relevant project or communication context.
- Potential conflicts of interest.
- Whether immediate protective measures are necessary.

### Stage 3 — Opportunity to Provide Context

Where appropriate and safe, the people involved may be invited to provide relevant information.

The process should avoid unnecessary confrontation or direct contact between parties where that could increase risk.

Not every report requires direct interaction between the reporter and the person concerned.

### Stage 4 — Decision

Under the current governance model, the founder and maintainer determines the internal outcome, except where an explicitly authorized alternative arrangement has been established.

The decision should consider:

- The available evidence.
- The seriousness and context of the behaviour.
- The impact on affected individuals.
- Relevant previous conduct where appropriate.
- Potential conflicts of interest.
- Proportionality of the response.

A decision should not be presented as independent where the reviewer has a direct conflict of interest.

### Stage 5 — Follow-up

Where appropriate, the outcome may be communicated to the relevant parties.

The maintainer may also consider:

- Additional safeguards.
- Access restrictions.
- Communication boundaries.
- Removal of inappropriate content.
- Changes to participation arrangements.
- Other measures needed to reduce recurrence.

### 9.1 Timeframes

The Lab does not guarantee a fixed acknowledgement, investigation, or resolution timeframe.

The handling of a report depends on its seriousness, complexity, available information, and the resources available to the maintainer.

### 9.2 Appeals and Independent Review

There is currently no guaranteed formal internal appeal mechanism or independent disciplinary body.

Where the maintainer has a direct conflict, the limitation must be acknowledged. An independent reviewer may be considered where feasible and appropriately agreed, but such a reviewer must not be assumed to exist in advance.

Relevant external platform, contractual, or legal channels remain available where applicable.

## 10. Enforcement Measures

Violations may result in one or more measures, depending on their severity, context, and potential impact.

### 10.1 Informal Guidance

A participant may receive a private request to clarify or correct behaviour that appears inconsistent with this Code.

### 10.2 Formal Warning

A participant may receive a clear warning describing:

- The behaviour concerned.
- The relevant expectations.
- The changes required.
- The potential consequences of further violations.

### 10.3 Content Moderation

Inappropriate content may be edited, hidden, removed, or otherwise moderated where the relevant platform and permissions allow.

### 10.4 Temporary Restriction

Access to specific discussions, repositories, collaboration spaces, or activities may be temporarily restricted.

### 10.5 Removal of Access

A participant may lose repository access, collaboration privileges, or permission to participate in Lab activities.

### 10.6 Permanent Exclusion

For serious or repeated misconduct, a participant may be permanently excluded from Lab spaces and activities.

### 10.7 External Reporting

Where appropriate, suspected illegal activity, credible threats, or other serious matters may be referred to the relevant platform or competent authorities.

The Lab is not a substitute for emergency services, legal counsel, or law enforcement.

### 10.8 No Automatic Penalty

Enforcement measures should not be imposed solely because:

- A contributor criticises a decision.
- A proposal is rejected.
- A technical disagreement remains unresolved.
- A good-faith report cannot be substantiated.
- A participant expresses a different research or creative position.

The substance and context of the conduct must be considered.

## 11. Proportionality and Serious Incidents

Not every violation requires the same response.

The maintainer should consider:

- Severity and potential harm.
- Frequency and persistence.
- Intent and circumstances.
- Previous conduct where relevant.
- Willingness to acknowledge and correct the behaviour.
- Risk of recurrence.
- Impact on affected individuals and the wider Lab environment.
- Relevant conflicts of interest.
- Applicable platform or legal requirements.

Minor misunderstandings may be resolved through clarification.

Serious harassment, threats, deliberate exposure of private information, retaliation, or repeated abuse may justify immediate restrictions or exclusion without prior informal warnings.

Protective measures may be taken before an assessment is complete when reasonably necessary to reduce immediate risk.

Such measures should not automatically be presented as a final determination that misconduct occurred.

---

## 12. Good-Faith Reporting and Fair Treatment

The Lab encourages people to report concerns without fear of retaliation.

A report made in good faith should not result in punishment simply because it cannot be substantiated.

The process should respect the rights and dignity of all parties.

This means:

- Reports should be assessed on their substance.
- Conclusions should not be predetermined.
- Relevant context should be considered.
- Personal disagreement should not be treated as proof of misconduct.
- Confidential information should be protected.
- Enforcement should be proportionate.
- Retaliation should not be tolerated.
- Affected parties should have an appropriate opportunity to provide relevant context where safe and reasonable.

Knowingly false or malicious reports may be considered misconduct. An honest mistake, incomplete information, or an unsubstantiated allegation is not, by itself, evidence of malicious intent.

The purpose of this process is to maintain a healthy collaborative environment, not to suppress legitimate criticism or differences of opinion.

---

## 13. Conflicts Involving the Maintainer

The same behavioural expectations apply to the founder and maintainer.

A person raising a concern about the maintainer should not be expected to treat the maintainer's position as a reason to remain silent.

Where the concern involves the maintainer directly:

- The conflict of interest should be recognized.
- The maintainer should avoid presenting their own assessment as independent review.
- An independent reviewer may be considered where feasible and appropriate.
- Relevant platform reporting mechanisms may be used where applicable.
- External contractual or legal channels may be relevant.
- Limitations in the review process should be communicated honestly.

Because the Lab does not currently operate an independent governance or complaints body, it cannot guarantee independent adjudication of every report involving the maintainer.

This limitation does not remove the obligation to take concerns seriously.

The existence of founder-led authority must not be used to justify retaliation, intimidation, or dismissal of legitimate concerns.

---

## 14. Application to Contributors and Collaborators

By participating in Lab activities, contributors and collaborators are expected to follow this Code.

This includes people submitting:

- Code.
- Research.
- Experiments.
- Technical documentation.
- Creative work.
- Design proposals.
- Reviews and feedback.
- Other forms of collaboration.

Acceptance of a contribution does not exempt its author from this Code.

Repository access or technical responsibility does not exempt maintainers or contributors from behavioural expectations.

Disagreement with a maintainer decision does not justify harassment, retaliation, or abusive behaviour.

Likewise, the Lab's governance authority does not justify suppressing legitimate criticism or applying behavioural rules selectively.

Project-specific contribution requirements may add further expectations but must not undermine the principles established here.

## 16. Policy Review and Updates

This Code of Conduct should be reviewed when:

- The Lab's governance structure changes.
- New collaboration spaces or activities are introduced.
- The reporting process changes.
- New roles or responsibilities are established.
- Experience from actual conduct-related concerns identifies necessary improvements.
- Relevant legal, contractual, or platform requirements change.

The founder and maintainer is responsible for approving revisions under the current governance model.

Substantive revisions should update the document version and be recorded in version control.

Changes should be checked for consistency with the Lab Charter, Governance, Contribution Guidelines, and Security Policy.

## 17. Acknowledgment

Framing Creativity Lab is built on the belief that meaningful work emerges from both individual perspectives and constructive exchange.

A strong collaborative environment does not require uniform thinking.

It requires mutual respect, clear boundaries, intellectual honesty, and the ability to challenge ideas without diminishing people.

**Think freely. Challenge constructively. Collaborate responsibly.**
