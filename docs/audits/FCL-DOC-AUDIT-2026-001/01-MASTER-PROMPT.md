# MASTER PROMPT — FRAMING CREATIVITY LAB
# COMPREHENSIVE DOCUMENTATION AUDIT

## 1. ROLE AND MISSION

Act as a Senior Documentation Architect, Technical Governance Auditor, Software Engineering Standards Reviewer, and AI Policy Specialist.

Your mission is to conduct a comprehensive, evidence-based audit of the twelve foundational documentation files of **Framing Creativity Lab**.

This is not a proofreading exercise.

You must evaluate the documents as a connected governance, research, engineering, publication, and operational framework.

The objective is to determine whether the documentation is:

- Internally consistent.
- Structurally coherent.
- Technically accurate.
- Operationally meaningful.
- Clearly written.
- Properly scoped.
- Free from contradictions and unnecessary duplication.
- Appropriate for an independent, founder-led research and development laboratory.
- Ready for publication in a public GitHub repository.

You must identify weaknesses, ambiguities, omissions, contradictions, unrealistic commitments, and unnecessary complexity.

Do not assume that a document is correct simply because it is detailed or professionally written.

Audit its actual content.

---

## 2. PROJECT CONTEXT

**Organization:** Framing Creativity Lab  
**Repository:** framing-creativity-lab  
**Maintainer:** J-ART  
**Operating Model:** Independent, founder-led research and development laboratory  
**Primary Focus:** Artificial intelligence, creative technology, digital experiences, software engineering, experimental design, and applied research.

Framing Creativity Lab is an independent public-facing laboratory for experimentation, technical exploration, research, and selected publications.

It is not currently a large organization, a formal research institution, a foundation, or a company with established departments, employees, committees, or dedicated compliance teams.

The documentation must reflect this reality.

### Governance principles

- Founder-led decision-making.
- Clear and proportionate responsibilities.
- Independent research and experimentation.
- Responsible use of artificial intelligence.
- Technical quality and reproducibility.
- Controlled public publication.
- Protection of confidential and proprietary material.
- Transparent project maturity and limitations.
- Sustainable documentation and project management.

### Critical confidentiality boundary

The public Framing Creativity Lab repository must remain completely separate from proprietary, confidential, and restricted projects.

Do not search for, retrieve, reference, expose, reuse, or incorporate content from any private, proprietary, confidential, or otherwise sensitive project.

Do not inspect private repositories or external project directories as part of this audit.

Do not assume that an idea, implementation, architecture, asset, workflow, prompt, dataset, or technical solution from another project is authorized for publication.

The audit must be limited to the twelve target documents and the public repository context explicitly provided.

# 5. AUDIT DIMENSION A — INDIVIDUAL DOCUMENT QUALITY

Review each document independently.

Evaluate the following criteria.

### A1. Purpose and scope

- Is the purpose clearly defined?
- Is the scope sufficiently precise?
- Are responsibilities and limitations explicit?
- Is the document addressing the correct subject?
- Does it introduce concepts that belong elsewhere?
- Are important subjects missing?

### A2. Structural quality

- Is the document logically organized?
- Is the hierarchy of headings consistent?
- Are sections ordered appropriately?
- Are subsections necessary and proportionate?
- Are tables used meaningfully?
- Are checklists operational rather than decorative?
- Are there unnecessary repetitions?

### A3. Writing quality

- Is the English natural, professional, and precise?
- Are statements unambiguous?
- Is terminology consistent?
- Are sentences unnecessarily repetitive?
- Are generic statements replacing actionable requirements?
- Are claims appropriately qualified?
- Are recommendations distinguishable from mandatory requirements?

### A4. Operational usefulness

- Can someone understand how to apply the policy?
- Are responsibilities assigned?
- Are procedures sufficiently specific?
- Are acceptance criteria clear?
- Can the document be used without relying on undocumented assumptions?
- Does it make promises that the Lab cannot realistically fulfill?

### A5. Technical accuracy

- Are technical statements correct?
- Are software engineering concepts used appropriately?
- Are security recommendations meaningful?
- Are AI-specific risks adequately explained?
- Are research and evaluation methods credible?
- Are versioning and lifecycle practices appropriate?

### A6. Governance realism

Check whether the document assumes organizational capabilities that do not currently exist.

Identify references to:

- Nonexistent committees.
- Unestablished departments.
- Unassigned responsibilities.
- Dedicated security teams.
- Independent moderation bodies.
- Formal approval structures that have not been implemented.
- Guaranteed response times.
- Unsupported service-level commitments.
- Unfunded maintenance obligations.

The documentation must describe the actual operating model, not an imagined future organization.

### A7. Publication readiness

- Is all content appropriate for a public repository?
- Does it expose private or sensitive information?
- Does it contain placeholders?
- Does it contain inconsistent metadata?
- Does it make unsupported legal, security, or compliance claims?
- Are third-party references and external resources handled appropriately?

# 6. AUDIT DIMENSION B — CROSS-DOCUMENT CONSISTENCY

This is one of the most important parts of the audit.

Read all twelve documents together and check their consistency.

Do not limit the analysis to individual quality.

- Operating model.

- `CODE-OF-CONDUCT.md`
- `CONTRIBUTING.md`

- Who has final decision-making authority?
- Who approves contributions?
- Who authorizes publication?
- Who manages repository access?
- Who handles complaints?
- Who handles conflicts of interest?

- Are delegation rules consistent?

- `PUBLICATION-POLICY.md`
- `LAB-CHARTER.md`

- `CONTRIBUTING.md`

Verify:

- What is eligible for publication?
- Who authorizes publication?
- How are restricted materials classified?
- How are third-party assets handled?
- How are contributor rights protected?
- How are confidential projects kept separate?
- What happens when an unauthorized disclosure is identified?
- Are publication classifications used consistently?

Check whether the publication classifications P0, P1, P2, and P3 have identical meanings wherever they appear.

### B4. Research consistency

Compare:

- `RESEARCH-METHODOLOGY.md`
- `PROJECT-LIFECYCLE.md`
- `QUALITY-STANDARDS.md`

Verify that:

- Experimental procedures are aligned.
- Evidence and validation terminology is consistent.
- Failed experiments are treated appropriately.
- Reproducibility expectations are realistic.

### B5. Security consistency

Compare:

- `SECURITY.md`
- `PUBLICATION-POLICY.md`
- `QUALITY-STANDARDS.md`
- `RESPONSIBLE-AI.md`
- `PROJECT-TEMPLATE.md`

Verify:

- Vulnerability reporting procedures are consistent.
- Security review expectations are aligned.
- Credential handling rules are consistent.
- Risk classifications do not conflict.
- Incident response responsibilities are clear.
- Publication restrictions are aligned with security requirements.
- AI-specific threats are appropriately covered.

Check whether `SECURITY.md` assumes GitHub security functionality has been enabled when that has not been verified.

### B6. Responsible AI consistency

Compare:

- `RESPONSIBLE-AI.md`
- `RESEARCH-METHODOLOGY.md`
- `QUALITY-STANDARDS.md`
- `SECURITY.md`
- `PUBLICATION-POLICY.md`
- `PROJECT-TEMPLATE.md`

Verify:

- AI risk classifications are consistent.
- Human oversight requirements are realistic.
- Data classifications are aligned.
- AI evaluation criteria are compatible.
- Model documentation requirements are clear.
- Prompt and data security are covered.
- Autonomous agent permissions are constrained.
- AI-generated intellectual property is treated consistently.
- AI experimentation is clearly distinguished from operational deployment.

### B7. Lifecycle and quality consistency

Compare:

- `PROJECT-LIFECYCLE.md`
- `QUALITY-STANDARDS.md`
- `PROJECT-TEMPLATE.md`

Verify that:

- Project statuses use the same vocabulary.
- Maturity levels are distinct from lifecycle stages.
- Publication status is not confused with project maturity.
- Validation criteria correspond to quality levels.
- Project closure and archival procedures are aligned.
- The project template captures the information required by the lifecycle.
- The quality gates are consistent with publication requirements.

# 8. AUDIT DIMENSION D — DOCUMENT OVERLAP AND REDUNDANCY

Determine whether the twelve documents have clearly defined responsibilities.

A well-designed documentation system should avoid making every document repeat every policy.

Identify:

- Repeated explanations.
- Duplicated procedures.
- Conflicting definitions.
- Sections that belong in another document.
- Policies that are unnecessarily long.
- Areas requiring a single authoritative definition.
- Missing cross-references.

For every significant overlap, determine whether it is:

1. Necessary reinforcement.
2. Acceptable contextual repetition.
3. Unnecessary duplication.
4. Contradictory duplication.

Identify statements that:

**Operational Procedure** — What the Lab has actually established and can execute.

# 16. FINDING SEVERITY

Classify every material finding.

### CRITICAL

A problem that creates a substantial risk of:

- Confidential information disclosure.
- Incorrect publication authorization.
- Serious legal or intellectual property exposure.
- Dangerous security practices.
- Contradictory governance authority.
- Fundamentally misleading public claims.

### HIGH

A significant problem affecting:

- Policy consistency.
- Operational accountability.
- Security or privacy procedures.
- Publication controls.
- Technical reliability.
- Important project responsibilities.

### MEDIUM

A material but non-blocking issue involving:

- Ambiguous definitions.
- Inconsistent terminology.
- Unnecessary duplication.
- Missing procedural detail.
- Incomplete cross-references.
- Weak documentation structure.

### LOW

A minor improvement involving:

- Grammar.
- Formatting.
- Small wording inconsistencies.
- Heading conventions.
- Minor navigation improvements.

### INFORMATIONAL

A useful observation or future improvement that does not constitute a defect.

Do not classify every stylistic preference as a defect.

Prioritize actual consequences.

# 19. FINAL CONSTRAINTS

The following constraints are mandatory.

### Do not rewrite the documents during this audit.

The first objective is diagnosis, not remediation.

### Do not introduce unnecessary complexity.

Do not recommend a separate document for every possible topic.

### Do not invent an organization.

Keep governance consistent with an independent, founder-led laboratory.

### Do not assume operational capabilities.

A policy describing an action does not prove that the action has been configured or implemented.

### Do not weaken confidentiality.

Never expose private or proprietary project content.

### Do not invent legal compliance.

Identify legal questions requiring verification instead of claiming compliance.

### Do not force identical structures.

Each document should retain its own purpose, format, and appropriate level of detail.

### Preserve the Lab's identity.

The documentation should communicate an independent multidisciplinary research and development laboratory exploring AI, creative technology, digital experiences, experimental design, and software engineering.

### Preserve technical precision.

Do not replace meaningful technical terminology with generic corporate language.

# 20. FINAL EXECUTION INSTRUCTION

Begin by locating the twelve actual source files.

Read all twelve completely before drawing cross-document conclusions.

Then conduct the audit in the order defined above.

Do not stop after reviewing the README or the first few documents.

Do not prioritize stylistic corrections over structural, technical, governance, security, or publication-related weaknesses.

The final report must be comprehensive, specific, traceable, and actionable.

**The objective is not to make twelve documents look consistent. The objective is to establish twelve documents that work together as one coherent, credible, and operational documentation framework for Framing Creativity Lab.**
