# 01 — PURPOSE OF THIS CHARTER

This charter establishes the identity, mission, operating principles, governance framework and publication standards of Framing Creativity Lab.

It defines the conditions under which research, experiments, prototypes, tools, documentation and other technical or creative work may be developed, evaluated and published under the lab's identity.

Its purpose is to ensure that the lab remains:

- Independent in its research and technical direction.
- Consistent in its approach to experimentation and development.
- Transparent about its methods, results and limitations.
- Responsible in its use of artificial intelligence and emerging technologies.
- Protective of intellectual property, privacy and confidential information.
- Organised around documented and reproducible work.
- Open to meaningful exploration without compromising technical integrity.

This document serves as the foundational reference for the lab's activities.

All projects published under the Framing Creativity Lab identity should respect the principles and requirements established here.

# 02 — IDENTITY & INDEPENDENCE

## 2.1 Identity

**Framing Creativity Lab** is an independent research and experimentation initiative established by J-ART.

It operates at the intersection of:

- Artificial intelligence.
- Software engineering.
- Creative technology.
- Digital product development.
- Computational design.
- Interactive experiences.
- Experimental research.

The lab brings together technical and creative disciplines to investigate emerging possibilities, test practical approaches and develop documented implementations.

## 2.2 Independence

Framing Creativity Lab operates independently.

It is not, by default, a subsidiary, department, official research centre or public development division of another company, organisation or commercial project.

Its identity, documentation and published work must not imply institutional affiliations, partnerships, sponsorships or endorsements that have not been explicitly established.

The lab retains independence in its research priorities, technical decisions, publication choices and experimental direction.

## 2.3 Scope of independence

Independence does not imply isolation.

The lab may use third-party technologies, services, frameworks, models, platforms and development tools.

Such use does not imply endorsement, ownership or formal affiliation.

External resources must be acknowledged where appropriate, and their respective licences and terms must be respected.

# 03 — MISSION & OBJECTIVES

## 3.1 Mission

The mission of Framing Creativity Lab is to explore the relationship between technology, artificial intelligence and human creativity through practical research, technical experimentation and independent development.

The lab seeks to transform questions into hypotheses, hypotheses into experiments, and promising experiments into meaningful technical or creative outcomes.

## 3.2 Strategic objectives

The lab operates around six complementary objectives.

### A. Exploration

Investigate emerging technologies, computational methods, software architectures and creative approaches.

### B. Experimentation

Test concepts through controlled experiments, technical studies, prototypes and proofs of concept.

### C. Development

Build functional tools, applications, reusable components and experimental digital experiences.

### D. Evaluation

Assess technical feasibility, behaviour, reliability, usability and limitations.

### E. Documentation

Record methodologies, implementation decisions, observations and results.

### F. Knowledge Development

Build a structured body of technical and creative knowledge that can inform future investigations.

## 3.3 Research orientation

The lab is not required to pursue commercialisation for every experiment.

Research may be exploratory, conceptual, technical, methodological or application-oriented.

An experiment may be considered valuable even when it does not produce a finished application, provided that its purpose and findings are documented meaningfully.

# 05 — GOVERNANCE

## 5.1 Governance model

Framing Creativity Lab follows an independent, founder-led governance model.

The founder and maintainer is responsible for:

- Defining the lab's overall direction.
- Establishing research priorities.
- Approving the inclusion of projects.
- Maintaining the lab's identity and standards.
- Reviewing public releases.
- Managing repository organisation.
- Protecting confidential and proprietary information.
- Approving changes to this charter.

This model reflects the lab's current independent operating structure.

It does not imply the existence of a board, formal research committee or external supervisory body.

## 5.2 Decision-making

Decisions should be based on:

1. Alignment with the lab's mission.
2. Technical and methodological relevance.
3. Research or experimental value.
4. Feasibility and available resources.
5. Intellectual property and publication rights.
6. Privacy and security considerations.
7. Quality of documentation.

Not every proposed experiment needs to be accepted.

The maintainer may reject, postpone, restrict or discontinue work that falls outside the lab's scope or publication standards.

## 5.3 Project ownership

The inclusion of a project in this repository does not automatically transfer intellectual property ownership to Framing Creativity Lab.

Ownership, authorship, contributor rights and publication permissions must be established separately where relevant.

Contributions must not be presented in a way that misrepresents authorship or ownership.

# 06 — RESEARCH METHODOLOGY

The lab follows an experiment-driven methodology.

The general development cycle is:

**Research → Hypothesis → Design → Experiment → Prototype → Evaluation → Documentation → Publication**

## 6.1 Research

Identify the subject, investigate existing approaches and establish the relevant technical context.

Research should distinguish established information from assumptions, interpretations and hypotheses.

## 6.2 Hypothesis

Define what is being investigated and what the experiment intends to demonstrate or understand.

A hypothesis should be specific enough to guide the experiment.

## 6.3 Design

Select appropriate tools, technologies, methods and evaluation criteria.

The scope should be proportionate to the research question.

## 6.4 Experimentation

Implement the proposed approach under documented conditions.

Experiments should be structured sufficiently to make their outcomes understandable.

## 6.5 Prototyping

Where appropriate, develop a functional demonstration or technical implementation.

A prototype should be clearly distinguished from a finished or production-ready system.

## 6.6 Evaluation

Assess the results against the original hypothesis or objective.

Evaluation may include:

- Functional testing.
- Comparative analysis.
- Performance observations.
- Reliability assessment.
- Usability evaluation.
- Technical feasibility.
- Limitations and failure conditions.

Claims must be proportionate to the evidence available.

## 6.7 Documentation

Record the purpose, implementation, relevant decisions, dependencies, observations and limitations.

Documentation should allow another technically capable person to understand the work.

## 6.8 Publication

Only experiments that have passed the applicable publication checks may be released under the lab's public identity.

Publication is a separate decision from technical completion.

# 07 — PROJECT CLASSIFICATION

Each project should be assigned an appropriate category.

| Category | Definition |
|---|---|
| Research | Investigation of a technical or conceptual question. |
| Experiment | Exploration of a specific method or technology. |
| Proof of Concept | Implementation demonstrating the feasibility of an idea. |
| Prototype | Early functional representation of a proposed system. |
| Tool | Independent utility or reusable technical resource. |
| Framework | Reusable structure, methodology or development foundation. |
| Creative Study | Exploration of computational and interactive creativity. |
| Documentation | Technical specifications, methods and research references. |

Projects should also communicate their maturity.

Recommended maturity labels:

- `Research`
- `Exploration`
- `Prototype`
- `Experimental`
- `Validated`
- `Stable`
- `Archived`

These labels must not be used interchangeably.

For example, a prototype is not automatically validated, and validation against a limited test case does not establish general production readiness.

# 08 — PUBLICATION POLICY

## 8.1 General principle

Publication is intentional, selective and subject to review.

The fact that a project exists, functions or has been developed using public technologies does not mean that it is automatically eligible for publication.

All public material must have an appropriate publication basis.

## 8.2 Publication requirements

Before a project is published, the following questions must be addressed:

- Does it align with the lab's mission?
- Is the purpose of the project clearly described?
- Is the material authorised for public distribution?
- Are all third-party resources appropriately attributed?
- Have confidential data and proprietary information been excluded?
- Have credentials and sensitive configuration values been removed?
- Is the repository structure understandable?
- Are dependencies and execution requirements documented?
- Are known limitations communicated?
- Is the selected licence or rights statement appropriate?

Projects that do not satisfy relevant requirements must remain unpublished until the issues are resolved.

## 8.3 Publication completeness

Depending on its complexity, a public project should contain:

- A descriptive README.
- A clear project objective.
- A project status.
- An explanation of the implementation.
- Installation or usage instructions.
- Dependency information.
- Relevant testing instructions.
- Known limitations.
- Appropriate attribution.
- Licensing information or an explicit rights statement.

Not every small experiment requires extensive documentation, but every release must provide enough information to prevent misleading expectations.

## 8.4 Publication independence

A public experiment must be independently publishable.

It must not depend on access to confidential systems, private commercial infrastructure or undisclosed project assets.

Public documentation must not expose the existence, structure or operational details of confidential work unless such disclosure has been explicitly authorised.

# 09 — INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY & CONFIDENTIALITY

This section establishes one of the lab's most important operational boundaries.

## 9.1 Separation of public and proprietary work

Framing Creativity Lab must remain separate from proprietary, commercial and confidential development environments.

No private commercial repository should be mirrored, copied or transferred into the lab merely to make it publicly visible.

This includes source code, project documentation, architecture diagrams, internal specifications, design assets, workflows, business logic and other proprietary materials.

## 9.2 Protected project environments

Projects managed outside the public lab may contain:

- Commercial product development.
- Confidential product architecture.
- Private source code.
- Client-related work.
- Internal research.
- Unreleased product specifications.
- Private AI configurations.
- Proprietary datasets.
- Non-public business processes.
- Private design systems and technical assets.

Such material is outside the public scope of this repository unless a separate and explicit publication decision has been made.

## 9.3 Independent implementations

A public experiment may investigate a general technical concept that is also relevant to private or commercial work.

However, the public implementation must be independently developed and reviewed.

It must not contain extracted proprietary implementation details, confidential data, private configurations or protected assets.

The similarity of a general concept does not grant permission to reuse its proprietary implementation.

## 9.4 No indirect disclosure

Confidentiality is not limited to source code.

Sensitive information may also be revealed through:

- Repository names and descriptions.
- Commit messages and history.
- Issue discussions.
- Screenshots and demonstrations.
- Configuration examples.
- Documentation.
- Generated files and metadata.
- API endpoints and infrastructure references.
- Architecture diagrams.
- Model prompts and internal workflows.

All public materials must be considered in their complete context.

## 9.5 Accidental disclosure

If sensitive information is discovered in a public repository:

1. Publication or further distribution should be stopped where possible.
2. The affected material must be assessed.
3. Exposed credentials must be revoked or rotated immediately when applicable.
4. Git history and related releases must be examined.
5. Removal and remediation must be performed using appropriate procedures.
6. Any relevant third-party or affected-party notification obligations must be assessed.

Simply deleting a sensitive file from the latest commit does not necessarily remove it from repository history or existing copies.

## 9.6 Ownership and permissions

The lab does not claim ownership of third-party intellectual property.

All published materials must have an appropriate basis for publication, including original authorship, permission, an applicable licence or another valid legal basis.

# 10 — ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE RESPONSIBILITY

AI-related experiments must follow principles of transparency, critical evaluation and responsible use.

## 10.1 Transparency

Where AI materially contributes to an experiment, the relevant contribution should be disclosed when useful to understanding or reproducing the work.

This may include:

- Model or provider.
- Version or configuration.
- Prompting methodology.
- Data and input characteristics.
- Human intervention.
- Automated processing steps.

## 10.2 Output evaluation

AI-generated content must not automatically be treated as accurate or verified.

Where results are used to support technical or factual claims, appropriate validation should be performed.

## 10.3 Data handling

Private, personal, confidential or restricted data must not be submitted to AI systems without an appropriate basis and adequate consideration of the relevant privacy and security requirements.

Public experiments should prefer synthetic, public-domain, properly licensed or otherwise authorised datasets.

## 10.4 Attribution

AI assistance should be described accurately when its contribution is material.

Human responsibility for design decisions, validation, publication and project integrity remains with the responsible author or maintainer.

## 10.5 Limitations

Experiments should document relevant limitations, including:

- Hallucinations and factual errors.
- Model variability.
- Inconsistent outputs.
- Bias and representational limitations.
- Dependence on external services.
- Latency and operational costs.
- Security and privacy constraints.

No AI system should be described as reliable, unbiased or autonomous without evidence supporting the specific claim.

# 11 — TECHNICAL QUALITY & ENGINEERING STANDARDS

Published projects should follow engineering practices appropriate to their scope and maturity.

## 11.1 Architecture

Implementations should favour:

- Clear separation of responsibilities.
- Understandable project organisation.
- Explicit dependencies.
- Consistent naming conventions.
- Reusable structures where appropriate.
- Maintainable code.
- Minimal unnecessary complexity.

## 11.2 Development practices

Where applicable, projects should include:

- Dependency manifests and lockfiles.
- Environment configuration examples.
- Input validation.
- Error handling.
- Appropriate logging.
- Unit or integration tests.
- Build and execution instructions.
- Version control conventions.

## 11.3 Security

Projects must not include:

- API keys.
- Access tokens.
- Passwords.
- Private certificates.
- Production credentials.
- Unauthorised personal information.
- Confidential configuration files.

Security-related examples should use placeholders or local configuration methods rather than real credentials.

## 11.4 Accessibility and usability

Interactive applications should consider accessibility and usability appropriate to their intended audience.

Relevant considerations include:

- Semantic HTML.
- Keyboard navigation.
- Focus visibility.
- Readable contrast.
- Responsive layouts.
- Reduced-motion preferences.
- Clear interaction feedback.
- Accessible form controls.

## 11.5 Technical claims

Performance, security, accessibility, reliability and compatibility claims should be supported by identifiable testing or evidence.

The lab should avoid presenting assumptions as verified results.

# 12 — DATA, PRIVACY & EXTERNAL SERVICES

Research involving datasets, external APIs or hosted services must consider the rights and conditions governing those resources.

Before publication, the project should establish:

- The origin of relevant data.
- Whether its use is permitted.
- Whether redistribution is permitted.
- Whether personal or sensitive information is involved.
- Whether external services process submitted information.
- Whether the experiment introduces third-party dependencies.
- Whether the project requires credentials or paid services.

Published examples should avoid exposing private user data and should explain relevant external service requirements.

The lab does not guarantee the availability, accuracy or continued operation of external services.

---

# 13 — THIRD-PARTY TECHNOLOGIES & ATTRIBUTION

Framing Creativity Lab may incorporate third-party software, models, frameworks, assets and services.

Their use must respect applicable terms and licences.

Projects should identify relevant external dependencies and provide attribution where required.

Particular attention should be given to:

- Open-source software licences.
- Model and dataset licences.
- Commercial API terms.
- Image, audio and font usage rights.
- Creative Commons conditions.
- Redistribution restrictions.
- Attribution and derivative-work requirements.

The presence of a third-party resource in a project does not automatically permit its redistribution.

Where licence compatibility or ownership is uncertain, the material must be reviewed before publication.

# 14 — CONTRIBUTIONS & COLLABORATION

## 14.1 Current operating model

The lab is currently maintained independently by J-ART.

External contributions are not automatically accepted across the repository.

## 14.2 Project-specific collaboration

Future collaboration may be considered on an individual project basis.

Each collaborative project should define:

- Its objectives and scope.
- Responsibilities and authorship.
- Contribution expectations.
- Intellectual property arrangements.
- Licensing and publication conditions.
- Confidentiality requirements.
- Decision-making responsibilities.

## 14.3 Contribution integrity

Contributors must not submit material that they do not have the right to publish.

Contributions must not introduce confidential code, unauthorised data, proprietary third-party assets or undisclosed dependencies.

## 14.4 Recognition

Authorship and meaningful contributions should be acknowledged accurately.

Project documentation should distinguish original work, external contributions and third-party resources.

# 15 — DOCUMENTATION & KNOWLEDGE MANAGEMENT

Documentation is a fundamental component of the lab's methodology.

Its purpose is to preserve technical understanding, communicate decisions and make experimentation more reproducible.

## 15.1 Documentation principles

Documentation should be:

- Accurate.
- Structured.
- Relevant.
- Understandable.
- Maintained when necessary.
- Proportionate to the scope of the project.

## 15.2 Project documentation

Substantial experiments should document:

1. Project identity and objective.
2. Research question or hypothesis.
3. Technical context.
4. Technologies and dependencies.
5. Implementation.
6. Testing and evaluation.
7. Results and observations.
8. Limitations.
9. Reproduction instructions.
10. Attribution and licensing.

## 15.3 Knowledge preservation

Important findings should not remain exclusively in informal conversations, temporary notes or undocumented development environments.

Relevant knowledge should be transferred into appropriate project documentation when it is intended to support future work.

# 16 — PROJECT LIFECYCLE

Projects may evolve through the following lifecycle.

| Stage | Description |
|---|---|
| Proposed | An idea has been identified for possible investigation. |
| Research | Initial investigation and technical context gathering. |
| Exploration | Early testing of methods and technologies. |
| Development | Active implementation of the experiment. |
| Prototype | A functional early implementation exists. |
| Evaluation | Results are being tested against defined criteria. |
| Published | The project has passed its publication review. |
| Maintained | The published project receives ongoing attention. |
| Archived | Active development has ended or been suspended. |

A project may move backwards in its lifecycle when new evidence, technical problems or changes in scope require additional investigation.

Publication does not imply permanent maintenance.

Archived work should retain a clear explanation of its status where appropriate.

# 17 — CHANGE MANAGEMENT

This charter may evolve as the lab develops.

Changes should preserve the original purpose and operating principles while allowing the framework to respond to new technologies, requirements and organisational needs.

## 17.1 Change authority

The maintainer is responsible for approving changes to this document.

## 17.2 Versioning

The charter uses semantic document versioning.

- **Major version:** Significant change to governance, mission or fundamental principles.
- **Minor version:** Addition or substantive revision of a policy or operating procedure.
- **Patch version:** Editorial corrections, clarification and non-substantive improvements.

## 17.3 Change records

Substantive revisions should be recorded with:

- Version number.
- Date.
- Summary of changes.
- Reason for revision.

Previous versions may be retained in version control for historical reference.

# 18 — INTERPRETATION & APPLICATION

This charter establishes the lab's internal operating standards.

It should be interpreted consistently with the lab's mission and the principle of responsible publication.

Where a project-specific specification introduces additional requirements, those requirements should be documented separately.

Project-specific rules may impose stricter requirements but should not be used to bypass the fundamental confidentiality, intellectual property and publication principles established here.

Where a situation is unclear, the safer course is to withhold publication until the relevant rights, permissions or technical conditions have been clarified.

This charter is an operating and governance document. It does not replace applicable legislation, contractual obligations, third-party licences or formal legal advice.

# 19 — FOUNDATIONAL PRINCIPLES

The lab is guided by the following principles:

**Independence**  
Research and experimentation should remain intellectually and technically independent.

**Curiosity**  
Questions and exploration are essential to meaningful innovation.

**Rigor**  
Technical claims should be supported by evidence and appropriate evaluation.

**Creativity**  
Technology should create opportunities for new forms of expression and experience.

**Transparency**  
Methods, assumptions, limitations and contributions should be communicated honestly.

**Responsibility**  
Research and publication should respect privacy, intellectual property, security and human interests.

**Quality**  
Published work should demonstrate care in implementation, documentation and presentation.

**Restraint**  
Not everything developed needs to be published. Selectivity is part of maintaining the integrity of the lab.

# 20 — CLOSING STATEMENT

Framing Creativity Lab exists to explore what becomes possible when creative thinking, technical understanding and emerging technologies meet.

Its value is not measured solely by the number of repositories, prototypes or tools it produces.

It is also measured by the quality of the questions it investigates, the discipline of its experiments, the clarity of its findings and the responsibility with which it shares its work.

The lab recognises that meaningful innovation requires both freedom to explore and discipline to evaluate.

It seeks to maintain that balance.

**Explore without assuming.  
Experiment without obscuring.  
Build with intention.  
Evaluate with rigor.  
Publish with responsibility.**
