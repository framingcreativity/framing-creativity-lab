# FRAMING CREATIVITY LAB

### Independent Research · Artificial Intelligence · Creative Technology · Digital Innovation

**Exploring the intersection of human creativity, artificial intelligence, technology and digital experiences.**

Framing Creativity Lab is an independent research and experimentation environment dedicated to exploring, designing and building at the intersection of artificial intelligence, software engineering, creative technology and digital design.

The lab investigates how emerging technologies can be translated into meaningful applications, functional systems, interactive experiences and new creative possibilities.

It brings together conceptual research, technical exploration, product thinking and practical experimentation.

Rather than approaching technology exclusively as a means of solving problems, the lab also considers it a medium for exploration, expression, transformation and innovation.

The objective is to move from ideas to working experiments, from technical exploration to practical applications, and from isolated concepts to documented, reproducible work.

---

## 01 — MISSION

The mission of Framing Creativity Lab is to explore and develop new ways of combining technology, intelligence and creativity.

The lab focuses on understanding emerging technologies, testing their practical capabilities, investigating their limitations and translating relevant discoveries into tangible outcomes.

Its work is guided by five objectives:

- **Explore** — Investigate emerging technologies, methodologies, tools and technical possibilities.
- **Experiment** — Test ideas through prototypes, proofs of concept, technical studies and interactive demonstrations.
- **Build** — Transform promising concepts into functional applications, reusable components and practical tools.
- **Understand** — Document technical decisions, observations, limitations and lessons learned.
- **Connect** — Explore the relationships between artificial intelligence, human creativity, digital systems and user experience.

The emphasis is not on producing technology for its own sake, but on understanding what technology makes possible and determining where it creates genuine value.

## 02 — VISION

Technology and creativity are often approached as separate disciplines.

One focuses on systems, structures, logic and performance. The other explores interpretation, perception, imagination and expression.

Framing Creativity Lab operates between these perspectives.

It approaches technology as both an engineering discipline and a creative medium.

Artificial intelligence, for example, is not considered only as an automation mechanism. It is also an emerging layer of interaction, knowledge processing, experimentation and creative production.

Similarly, software is not simply a collection of functions. Its architecture, interface, behaviour and interaction model all contribute to the experience it creates.

The lab seeks to explore this relationship and develop approaches in which technical rigour and creative thinking reinforce one another.

**The central question is not only what can be built, but what becomes possible when different disciplines meet.**

## 04 — APPROACH & METHODOLOGY

Framing Creativity Lab follows an experiment-driven approach.

Projects are developed progressively, with an emphasis on clarity, validation and documentation.

### Research → Hypothesis → Experiment → Prototype → Evaluation → Documentation

**1. Research**

Define the subject, investigate existing approaches and identify relevant technologies, methods or references.

**2. Hypothesis**

Establish the question being investigated, the intended outcome and the assumptions behind the experiment.

**3. Experimentation**

Test the idea through technical exploration, small implementations, comparisons or controlled experiments.

**4. Prototyping**

Develop a functional representation whenever practical. This may take the form of a script, interface, application, visual system, technical demonstration or proof of concept.

**5. Evaluation**

Examine the outcome against the original objective.

Evaluate functionality, usability, technical feasibility, reliability, limitations and potential improvements.

**6. Documentation**

Record the implementation, tools, dependencies, relevant decisions, results and known limitations.

Not every experiment needs to become a finished product. Some investigations are valuable precisely because they reveal constraints, expose weaknesses or demonstrate why a particular approach is not suitable.

Negative results and technical limitations are part of the research process.

## 06 — TECHNICAL PRINCIPLES

The lab follows a set of practical principles intended to maintain quality across different experiments and technologies.

### Clarity over unnecessary complexity

Solutions should address the problem without introducing avoidable architectural or technical overhead.

### Modularity and reusability

Where appropriate, implementations should favour clear separation of responsibilities, reusable components and understandable structures.

### Reproducibility

Experiments should provide sufficient information for another person to understand the implementation and reproduce its main results whenever technically possible.

### Documentation as part of development

Documentation is not an afterthought. It is part of the research and engineering process.

### Evaluation over assumption

Technical decisions should be supported by testing, observation or explicit reasoning rather than unsupported claims.

### Responsible use of AI

AI-assisted development and AI-generated outputs should be evaluated critically. Generated results should not automatically be treated as correct, reliable or production-ready.

### Accessibility and usability

Digital experiences should consider semantic structure, keyboard interaction, readability, responsive behaviour and accessibility from the beginning.

### Security and privacy

Credentials, private data, confidential information and sensitive operational details must never be included in public experiments.

## 07 — AI EXPERIMENTATION & EVALUATION

Artificial intelligence is one of the lab's principal research domains.

Experiments involving AI should document the relevant context sufficiently to make their results understandable.

Depending on the experiment, documentation may include:

- The problem or use case being investigated.
- The model, provider or technical framework used.
- The model version or configuration, when available.
- The prompting or interaction methodology.
- The input and output structure.
- Evaluation criteria and testing conditions.
- Observed strengths and weaknesses.
- Known limitations and failure cases.
- Cost, latency and resource considerations, where relevant.
- Human intervention or validation requirements.

AI-generated outputs should be distinguished from independently verified results.

Where model behaviour is non-deterministic, this should be acknowledged.

The lab does not assume that integrating an AI model automatically produces an intelligent, reliable or useful product. The quality of the surrounding architecture, data, interaction model and evaluation process remains essential.

## 09 — EXPERIMENT DOCUMENTATION STANDARD

Each substantial experiment should include a dedicated README or equivalent documentation.

Depending on its scope, the documentation should identify:

**Project identity**
- Name and short description.
- Research domain.
- Current development status.
- Main objective.

**Research context**
- Problem or question.
- Motivation.
- Initial hypothesis.
- Scope and assumptions.

**Technical implementation**
- Technologies and frameworks.
- Architecture and components.
- Dependencies and configuration.
- Installation and execution instructions.

**Results**
- Demonstrated functionality.
- Observations and evaluation.
- Screenshots or demonstrations, when relevant.
- Limitations and unresolved issues.

**Reproducibility**
- Required environment.
- Configuration examples.
- Data and resource requirements.
- Testing instructions.

**Publication**
- Relevant attribution.
- Licensing information.
- Security and privacy considerations.

Documentation should be proportional to the complexity of the experiment.

## 10 — DEVELOPMENT & QUALITY STANDARDS

The lab does not prescribe a single programming language, framework or technology stack.

The technical choices should follow the requirements of each experiment.

However, published implementations should aim to respect the following standards:

- Clear project structure and naming conventions.
- Explicit dependency management.
- Documented installation and execution procedures.
- Appropriate input validation and error handling.
- Separation of configuration and source code.
- Environment variables for sensitive configuration.
- No committed API keys, tokens, passwords or private credentials.
- Appropriate testing and validation.
- Clear attribution of external resources and dependencies.
- Documented limitations and known issues.
- Consistent version control practices.

Where a project involves a build or deployment process, the relevant procedures should be documented.

Where appropriate, automated checks may be introduced to validate the quality and consistency of the implementation.

## 11 — PRIVACY, SECURITY & PROJECT BOUNDARIES

Framing Creativity Lab is an independent public research environment.

Only material intentionally prepared and approved for public release belongs here.

The lab does not serve as a public mirror, development environment or distribution channel for proprietary commercial projects, client work or confidential material.

The following information must not be published:

- Private repository contents or source code.
- Proprietary application architectures or business logic.
- Client information and private datasets.
- Credentials, API keys and access tokens.
- Internal operational documentation.
- Confidential product specifications.
- Private prompts, workflows or technical assets.
- Third-party material without appropriate publication rights.

Any experiment derived from a broader research or development activity must be independently reviewed before publication.

Changing a filename, removing a brand name or extracting a small code fragment does not automatically make proprietary material safe to publish.

**Public availability must always be an intentional decision.**
