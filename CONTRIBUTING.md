# 01 — PURPOSE

This document defines the contribution principles, development practices and collaboration procedures applicable to Framing Creativity Lab.

Its purpose is to establish a clear and consistent framework for individuals who wish to participate in the lab's research, experimentation, technical development or documentation activities.

It explains how to:

- Propose new research ideas and experiments.
- Report technical problems.
- Suggest improvements.
- Contribute code and documentation.
- Submit corrections and enhancements.
- Collaborate on independent projects.
- Respect intellectual property and confidentiality.
- Follow the lab's technical and documentation standards.
- Participate in project reviews and publication processes.

These guidelines are intended to make collaboration accessible while preserving the quality, independence and integrity of the lab.

**Contributions should expand the lab's capabilities without compromising its principles.**

---

# 02 — CONTRIBUTION PHILOSOPHY

Framing Creativity Lab values contributions that demonstrate curiosity, technical understanding, thoughtful experimentation and a willingness to share knowledge.

Collaboration should be constructive, transparent and respectful of different disciplines.

Contributions may originate from:

- Software developers.
- AI engineers and researchers.
- Creative technologists.
- UX/UI designers.
- Digital artists.
- Technical writers.
- Independent researchers.
- Students and technology enthusiasts.
- Other contributors whose expertise is relevant to a particular project.

Formal academic or professional credentials are not required for every type of contribution.

However, contributions must be appropriate to their subject and supported by sufficient context, evidence or documentation.

## 2.1 Core principles

### Quality over quantity

A small, well-documented contribution is more valuable than a large, poorly understood submission.

### Clarity over complexity

Changes should solve identifiable problems without introducing unnecessary technical overhead.

### Collaboration over competition

Contributors should focus on improving the work rather than claiming superiority or ownership over others.

### Evidence over assertion

Technical claims should be supported by testing, documentation or clearly identified reasoning.

### Respect for intellectual property

Contributors must only submit material they have the right to share.

### Transparency

Relevant assumptions, dependencies, limitations and external assistance should be disclosed.

### Constructive communication

Technical disagreements should be addressed through evidence, reasoning and respectful discussion.

# 03 — GOVERNANCE & CONTRIBUTION AUTHORITY

## 3.1 Current governance model

Framing Creativity Lab currently operates under an independent, founder-led governance model.

The maintainer, J-ART, retains responsibility for:

- Overall repository direction.
- Research priorities.
- Project acceptance.
- Contribution review.
- Technical and editorial standards.
- Repository organisation.
- Publication approval.
- Intellectual property and confidentiality controls.
- Final decisions concerning integration and release.

This structure reflects the lab's current operating model.

It does not imply the existence of a formal board, external research committee or collective governing body.

## 3.2 Contribution rights

Submitting a contribution does not automatically grant the contributor:

- Repository administration rights.
- Maintainer privileges.
- Decision-making authority.
- Publication approval authority.
- Ownership of unrelated project material.
- Rights over other contributors' work.

Permissions are granted explicitly and according to the needs of individual projects.

## 3.3 Review authority

The maintainer may:

- Approve a contribution.
- Request modifications.
- Request additional documentation.
- Reject a contribution.
- Defer a contribution for further investigation.
- Close a proposal that falls outside the lab's scope.

All decisions should be guided by the lab's mission, technical requirements and publication standards.

# 05 — BEFORE CONTRIBUTING

Before starting substantial work, contributors should first understand the lab's purpose and operating principles.

The following documents provide the foundational context:

| Document | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `README.md` | Lab identity and research domains |
| `LAB-CHARTER.md` | Mission, governance and fundamental principles |
| `PUBLICATION-POLICY.md` | Publication eligibility and controls |
| `RESEARCH-METHODOLOGY.md` | Research and experimental methods |
| `CONTRIBUTING.md` | Contribution and collaboration procedures |

Contributors should also review the documentation of the specific project they intend to modify.

## 5.1 Check existing work

Before proposing a new feature or experiment:

- Search for related projects.
- Review existing issues.
- Examine relevant documentation.
- Check whether the feature already exists.
- Identify potential dependencies.
- Consider whether the proposal fits the lab's scope.

This helps prevent duplicated effort and unnecessary complexity.

## 5.2 Discuss substantial changes

Major changes should be discussed before implementation.

Examples include:

- New research domains.
- New architectural foundations.
- Major dependencies.
- Changes to project scope.
- Significant API modifications.
- New external services.
- Changes affecting security or privacy.

Small corrections and straightforward documentation improvements may be submitted directly.

# 06 — PROPOSING A NEW PROJECT OR EXPERIMENT

New research proposals should provide enough information to assess their relevance and feasibility.

A proposal should include:

### Project identity

- Proposed name.
- Research domain.
- Short description.
- Proposed project type.

### Research context

- Problem or question.
- Motivation.
- Existing approaches.
- Expected contribution.

### Objectives

- Primary objective.
- Secondary objectives, if relevant.
- Intended outcome.

### Technical approach

- Proposed methodology.
- Technologies and frameworks.
- Relevant dependencies.
- Estimated complexity.

### Evaluation

- Expected results.
- Evaluation criteria.
- Testing approach.
- Known limitations.

### Publication considerations

- Ownership.
- Third-party resources.
- Data requirements.
- Security and privacy considerations.
- Intended publication scope.

## 6.1 Proposal assessment

The maintainer may assess proposals according to:

- Alignment with the lab's mission.
- Research value.
- Technical feasibility.
- Originality or practical relevance.
- Resource requirements.
- Existing alternatives.
- Publication eligibility.
- Maintenance implications.

A proposal may be accepted, revised, deferred or declined.

Acceptance of a proposal does not guarantee publication or implementation.

# 07 — ISSUE REPORTING

Issues should be reported with sufficient detail to support investigation.

## 7.1 Bug reports

A useful bug report should include:

- A concise title.
- A clear description.
- Relevant environment information.
- Steps to reproduce.
- Expected behaviour.
- Actual behaviour.
- Error messages or logs, where appropriate.
- Relevant screenshots or examples.
- Possible workarounds, if known.

## 7.2 Feature requests

Feature requests should explain:

- The problem being addressed.
- Why the feature would be useful.
- Expected behaviour.
- Relevant use cases.
- Potential implementation considerations.

Feature requests should focus on the problem rather than prescribe an unnecessarily restrictive implementation.

## 7.3 Research questions

Research-related issues should identify:

- The question being investigated.
- Existing context.
- Relevant references.
- Proposed methods.
- Expected research value.

## 7.4 Issue quality

Avoid:

- Duplicate reports.
- Vague descriptions.
- Unsupported accusations.
- Unnecessary personal information.
- Credentials or private data.
- Unrelated discussions.
- Requests outside the project's scope.

## 7.5 Sensitive issues

Security vulnerabilities and confidential information must not be reported through public issues.

Use the appropriate private reporting channel described in `SECURITY.md`, once established.

# 08 — DEVELOPMENT WORKFLOW

Contributions involving code should follow a structured development process.

## 8.1 Repository preparation

Before modifying code:

1. Review the project README.
2. Inspect the repository structure.
3. Understand relevant architecture and dependencies.
4. Identify existing conventions.
5. Confirm the issue or objective being addressed.
6. Establish the expected scope of the modification.

## 8.2 Forking and branching

Where external contributions are accepted, contributors should generally work through a fork or an explicitly authorised branch.

Recommended branch naming conventions:

| Prefix | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `feature/` | New functionality |
| `fix/` | Bug correction |
| `docs/` | Documentation |
| `refactor/` | Code restructuring |
| `test/` | Testing improvements |
| `research/` | Research experiments |
| `prototype/` | Prototype development |
| `chore/` | Maintenance tasks |

Examples:

- `feature/structured-ai-output`
- `fix/input-validation`
- `docs/installation-guide`
- `research/model-comparison`

Branch names should be descriptive and concise.

## 8.3 Focused changes

Each contribution should have a clear objective.

Avoid combining unrelated changes in a single pull request.

For substantial modifications, divide the work into smaller, independently reviewable steps.

## 8.4 Local validation

Before submitting changes:

- Verify the implementation.
- Run relevant tests.
- Check formatting and linting where configured.
- Review the final diff.
- Inspect generated files.
- Check for accidental configuration changes.
- Confirm that documentation remains accurate.

## 8.5 Pull requests

Pull requests should explain:

- What has changed.
- Why the change was made.
- Which issue or objective it addresses.
- How it was implemented.
- Which tests were performed.
- Any known limitations.
- Any relevant screenshots or demonstrations.

Contributors should keep pull requests focused and provide enough context for meaningful review.

# 09 — CODE QUALITY STANDARDS

Code contributions should reflect the technical maturity and conventions of the project.

## 9.1 General principles

- Prefer readable and maintainable implementations.
- Use descriptive names.
- Keep responsibilities clearly separated.
- Avoid unnecessary abstractions.
- Minimise duplicated logic.
- Validate external inputs.
- Handle errors appropriately.
- Avoid undocumented behaviour.
- Respect existing architecture unless a change is justified.

## 9.2 Dependencies

New dependencies should be justified.

Before introducing a dependency, consider:

- Whether the functionality already exists.
- Maintenance activity.
- Security history.
- Licence compatibility.
- Bundle or runtime impact.
- Dependency complexity.
- Long-term availability.

## 9.3 Configuration

Configuration should be explicit and documented.

Sensitive information must not be hardcoded.

Provide safe example configuration where appropriate.

## 9.4 Testing

Tests should be appropriate to the nature of the change.

Examples include:

- Unit tests.
- Integration tests.
- Functional tests.
- Regression tests.
- End-to-end tests.
- Performance tests.
- Accessibility checks.

Contributors must not claim that tests passed unless they were actually executed.

## 9.5 Generated code and AI-assisted development

AI-assisted development is permitted where appropriate.

However:

- Contributors remain responsible for submitted code.
- Generated implementations must be reviewed.
- Dependencies must be checked.
- Security implications must be considered.
- Tests must be performed.
- Unverified generated claims must not be presented as established facts.

AI assistance does not replace contributor responsibility.

# 10 — RESEARCH CONTRIBUTION STANDARDS

Research contributions must respect the methodology established in `RESEARCH-METHODOLOGY.md`.

## 10.1 Research integrity

Contributors must not:

- Fabricate results.
- Alter observations to support a hypothesis.
- Conceal relevant failures.
- Misrepresent test conditions.
- Present assumptions as findings.
- Misattribute sources.
- Claim independent verification without independent evaluation.

## 10.2 Source attribution

Research sources must be identified clearly.

Where applicable, include:

- Author.
- Title.
- Publication date.
- Source URL.
- Relevant page or section.
- Access date.

Direct quotations and third-party materials must be handled according to applicable rights and attribution requirements.

## 10.3 Experimental records

Substantial research contributions should document:

- Research objective.
- Methodology.
- Technical environment.
- Test conditions.
- Inputs and outputs.
- Results.
- Evaluation.
- Limitations.
- Conclusions.

## 10.4 AI research

AI-related contributions should identify relevant:

- Models.
- Providers.
- Versions.
- Prompts.
- Parameters.
- Evaluation criteria.
- Test cases.
- Human interventions.
- Known limitations.

Sensitive or proprietary prompts and configurations must not be submitted.

# 11 — DOCUMENTATION STANDARDS

Documentation is an integral part of a contribution.

## 11.1 Documentation principles

All documentation should be:

- Clear.
- Accurate.
- Structured.
- Consistent.
- Relevant.
- Accessible.
- Maintained when necessary.

## 11.2 Documentation changes

When code changes affect usage or behaviour, the corresponding documentation should be updated.

Examples include:

- Installation procedures.
- Configuration.
- API behaviour.
- Command-line usage.
- Dependencies.
- Screenshots.
- Known limitations.
- Architecture.

## 11.3 Language

English is the primary language for public technical documentation to support international collaboration.

Other languages may be included where they add value, provided terminology and meaning remain consistent.

## 11.4 Claims and maturity

Documentation must accurately describe the project's maturity.

Do not describe an experimental prototype as production-ready or a limited test as comprehensive validation.

# 12 — INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY & LICENSING

## 12.1 Contribution ownership

Contributors retain whatever rights they hold in their original contributions, subject to applicable law and any explicit agreements.

Submitting a contribution does not automatically transfer ownership to Framing Creativity Lab.

## 12.2 Permission to contribute

Contributors must have the legal right to submit the material they provide.

They must not submit:

- Employer-owned code without permission.
- Client-owned material without authorisation.
- Confidential commercial implementations.
- Unauthorised third-party assets.
- Restricted datasets.
- Code copied from sources with incompatible licensing conditions.

## 12.3 Licensing compatibility

Contributions must be compatible with the licensing and rights conditions of the destination project.

Where no licence has been established, contributors must not assume that public repository access authorises reuse or redistribution.

## 12.4 Third-party material

External code, assets, datasets and other resources must be identified.

Required licence notices and attribution must be preserved.

## 12.5 AI-generated contributions

Contributors must consider relevant model and provider terms when submitting AI-generated material.

They should disclose material AI assistance where relevant to understanding authorship, provenance or reproducibility.

## 12.6 Ownership uncertainty

Contributions with unresolved ownership or publication rights must not be merged or published.

# 13 — CONFIDENTIALITY, PRIVACY & SECURITY

The protection of sensitive information is mandatory.

## 13.1 Prohibited submissions

Contributors must never submit:

- Private credentials.
- API keys.
- Passwords.
- Access tokens.
- Private certificates.
- Confidential client data.
- Proprietary commercial code.
- Private product architecture.
- Internal operational documentation.
- Unauthorised personal information.
- Restricted datasets.
- Private infrastructure configuration.

## 13.2 Independent work

Contributions must be independently publishable.

A public contribution must not expose confidential implementation details from another project or organisation.

## 13.3 Data handling

Use synthetic, public-domain, appropriately licensed or otherwise authorised data wherever practical.

Personal or sensitive information must not be included without an appropriate legal basis and approval.

## 13.4 Security concerns

Security vulnerabilities must be reported privately through the designated process.

Do not create public issues or pull requests that expose exploitable vulnerabilities.

## 13.5 Review and rejection

Contributions containing sensitive or unauthorised material may be rejected or removed.

The maintainer may request additional information where publication rights or security conditions are unclear.

# 14 — REVIEW & ACCEPTANCE PROCESS

All contributions are subject to review before integration.

## 14.1 Review dimensions

The review may consider:

**Relevance**
- Does the contribution align with the project?
- Does it address an identifiable need?

**Technical quality**
- Is the implementation appropriate?
- Is the code understandable?
- Are dependencies justified?

**Research integrity**
- Are claims and observations supported?
- Are methods and limitations documented?

**Security**
- Are sensitive values or unsafe behaviours present?
- Are external inputs handled appropriately?

**Licensing**
- Are publication and redistribution rights established?

**Documentation**
- Is the contribution sufficiently explained?

**Maintainability**
- Can the implementation reasonably be maintained?

## 14.2 Review outcomes

A contribution may receive one of the following outcomes:

- `Approved` — Accepted for integration.
- `Changes Requested` — Modifications are required.
- `Under Review` — Additional assessment is necessary.
- `Deferred` — The contribution may be reconsidered later.
- `Rejected` — The contribution will not be integrated.
- `Closed` — The discussion or submission has ended.

## 14.3 Changes requested

When modifications are requested, contributors should respond to the review comments and update their submission.

Unresolved issues may prevent acceptance.

## 14.4 Rejection

A rejection should be based on relevant project, technical, research, publication or governance considerations.

A contribution may be declined even if it is technically functional.

# 15 — COMMUNICATION & COLLABORATION

## 15.1 Communication principles

All participants should communicate professionally and respectfully.

Technical disagreement is acceptable and can improve the quality of research and implementation.

Personal attacks, harassment, discriminatory conduct and deliberately disruptive behaviour are not acceptable.

## 15.2 Discussion quality

Contributors should:

- Explain technical decisions.
- Provide relevant evidence.
- Ask focused questions.
- Distinguish facts from opinions.
- Acknowledge uncertainty.
- Respect different areas of expertise.
- Avoid unnecessary confrontation.

## 15.3 Responsiveness

Contributors should make reasonable efforts to respond to review questions and requests for clarification.

There is no guarantee of immediate review or integration.

## 15.4 Collaboration boundaries

Participation in a project does not automatically create a partnership, employment relationship, joint venture or formal organisational affiliation.

Any such arrangement must be established separately.

---

# 16 — CONTRIBUTOR RECOGNITION & ATTRIBUTION

Meaningful contributions should be recognised accurately.

Recognition may take the form of:

- Git commit attribution.
- Pull request history.
- Contributor listings.
- Project documentation.
- Acknowledgements.
- Research credits.

Attribution should reflect actual contributions.

Submitting a minor correction does not automatically establish authorship of an entire project.

Similarly, the maintainer should not claim sole authorship of substantial work contributed by others.

Where authorship or credit is unclear, it should be discussed before publication.

# 17 — PROJECT-SPECIFIC CONTRIBUTION RULES

Individual projects may introduce additional contribution requirements.

These may include:

- Specific coding conventions.
- Required test coverage.
- Architecture constraints.
- Research documentation formats.
- Data-handling procedures.
- Additional security checks.
- Design and accessibility standards.
- Special licensing conditions.

Project-specific guidelines may introduce stricter requirements than this document.

They must not be used to bypass the lab's fundamental confidentiality, ownership and publication policies.

Where a project has no additional contribution documentation, these guidelines apply by default.

# 19 — CONTRIBUTION AGREEMENT

By submitting a contribution to Framing Creativity Lab, contributors acknowledge that:

1. They have the right to submit the material.
2. They have not knowingly included confidential or unauthorised content.
3. They have identified relevant third-party material.
4. They have provided accurate information about the contribution.
5. They understand that submission does not guarantee acceptance.
6. They understand that publication decisions remain subject to the lab's governance and publication policies.
7. They retain responsibility for the accuracy and legitimacy of their contribution.
8. They understand that contribution rights and project licensing are governed by the applicable project terms and separate agreements where relevant.

Submission of a contribution does not, by itself, transfer intellectual property ownership or establish an open-source licence.
