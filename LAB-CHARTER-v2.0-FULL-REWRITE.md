## 1. Purpose and Authority

This Charter establishes the identity, mission, foundational principles, and operating boundaries of Framing Creativity Lab (the Lab).

It explains what the Lab exists to investigate, the kind of work it may undertake, and the principles that govern the development, evaluation, and publication of that work.

The Charter is the Lab's foundational policy document. It does not replace the detailed procedures and requirements maintained in the Lab's specialist documents. Where a specialist policy governs a specific operational subject, that policy supplies the applicable requirements and procedures, provided that it remains consistent with the foundational principles of this Charter.

The Charter is intended to:

- Preserve the Lab's independent identity and research direction.
- Define its mission and principal research domains.
- Establish shared principles for research, experimentation, and development.
- Set clear boundaries for confidentiality, intellectual property, and public release.
- Require responsible, evidence-based communication of project status and results.
- Provide a stable foundation for the Lab's policies and project documentation.

The Charter applies to work presented as an activity or publication of Framing Creativity Lab. It does not, by itself, grant permission to publish third-party material or private work.

## 2. Identity and Independence

### 2.1 Identity

Framing Creativity Lab is an independent research and experimentation initiative founded and maintained by J-ART.

The Lab operates at the intersection of:

- Artificial intelligence and applied AI.
- Software engineering and digital systems.
- Creative technology and computational creativity.
- Digital product and experience engineering.
- Interactive media and experimental design.
- Research methods and emerging technologies.

The Lab brings technical and creative disciplines together to investigate emerging possibilities, test practical approaches, and develop documented outcomes.

### 2.2 Independence

The Lab maintains independent research priorities, technical decisions, publication choices, and experimental direction.

It is not, by default, a subsidiary, department, official research centre, or public development division of another company, organisation, institution, or commercial project. Its name, repository, and publications must not imply an affiliation, partnership, sponsorship, endorsement, or institutional status that has not been explicitly established.

### 2.3 Use of External Resources

Independence does not mean isolation. The Lab may use third-party technologies, models, software, frameworks, datasets, platforms, services, and infrastructure.

Use of an external resource does not imply endorsement, ownership, partnership, or formal affiliation. Applicable terms, licences, attribution requirements, and restrictions must be respected.

## 3. Mission and Objectives

### 3.1 Mission

The mission of Framing Creativity Lab is to explore the relationship between technology, artificial intelligence, and human creativity through independent research, practical experimentation, and responsible development.

The Lab seeks to turn questions into hypotheses, hypotheses into experiments, and promising experiments into meaningful technical, methodological, or creative outcomes.

### 3.2 Strategic Objectives

The Lab's work is guided by six complementary objectives:

**Explore** — Investigate emerging technologies, computational methods, software architectures, and creative approaches.

**Experiment** — Test concepts through structured experiments, technical studies, prototypes, and proofs of concept.

**Build** — Develop functional tools, applications, reusable components, and experimental digital experiences where appropriate.

**Evaluate** — Assess feasibility, behaviour, reliability, usability, limitations, and results against declared criteria.

**Document** — Record methods, implementation decisions, observations, evidence, and limitations.

**Develop Knowledge** — Build a traceable body of technical and creative knowledge that can inform future investigations.

### 3.3 Non-commercial Research Value

The Lab is not required to commercialise every investigation. Research may be exploratory, conceptual, technical, methodological, or application-oriented.

An experiment may be valuable even when it does not produce a finished application. A negative result, a demonstrated limitation, or a well-supported conclusion that an approach is unsuitable may constitute a valid outcome when documented accurately.

## 4. Research and Experimentation Domains

The Lab's domains are interconnected areas of investigation, not mutually exclusive organisational departments.

### 4.1 Artificial Intelligence and Applied AI

Research into the capabilities, applications, integration, evaluation, and limitations of artificial intelligence. Areas may include:

- Large Language Models and model interaction.
- AI-assisted workflows and automation.
- Prompt engineering and structured instruction design.
- AI-enabled applications and agentic workflows.
- Retrieval-Augmented Generation and knowledge systems.
- Model evaluation, benchmarking, and output validation.
- Human-in-the-loop methods.
- Reliability, security, privacy, and operational constraints.

### 4.2 Creative Technology and Computational Creativity

Research into technology as a medium for creative exploration. Areas may include:

- Creative coding and generative systems.
- Computational and algorithmic creativity.
- Procedural and interactive design.
- Experimental visual and audiovisual systems.
- Digital composition and interactive media.
- Creative applications of artificial intelligence.

### 4.3 Digital Product and Experience Engineering

Research into the conception, design, and implementation of digital products and experiences. Areas may include:

- Product and application architecture.
- User experience and interface engineering.
- Design systems and reusable components.
- Interaction design and motion behaviour.
- Prototyping and proof-of-concept methods.
- Accessibility, usability, and responsive behaviour.

### 4.4 Software Architecture and Engineering

Research into the design and implementation of understandable, maintainable digital systems. Areas may include:

- Modular application architecture.
- APIs, integrations, and data modelling.
- Development tooling and automation.
- Build, testing, and deployment workflows.
- Reusability, maintainability, and technical quality.
- Performance, reliability, and security.

### 4.5 Emerging Technology and Independent Research

Exploration of technologies, methods, and questions that may introduce new technical or creative possibilities. This domain accommodates work that does not fit neatly within a predefined application category.

Research priorities may evolve as technologies, methods, and opportunities change. Such evolution does not require the Lab to claim expertise or operational capability beyond the evidence available.

## 5. Foundational Principles

All Lab activities should be guided by the following principles.

### 5.1 Independence

Research direction and technical judgement should remain independent of undisclosed external interests or unauthorised influence.

### 5.2 Curiosity and Intellectual Freedom

The Lab should allow meaningful questions and exploratory work across disciplinary boundaries. Exploration should not be restricted to ideas that are already expected to produce a product or commercial outcome.

### 5.3 Rigor and Evidence

Technical and research claims should be proportionate to the methods, testing, and evidence that support them. Assumptions, hypotheses, observations, and verified findings should not be presented as interchangeable.

### 5.4 Creativity

Technology may be approached both as an engineering discipline and as a medium for expression, interpretation, and experience.

### 5.5 Transparency

The Lab should communicate purpose, methods, relevant dependencies, contributions, results, and limitations clearly. Transparency does not override privacy, security, confidentiality, contractual obligations, or intellectual property rights.

### 5.6 Responsibility

Research and publication should consider their effects on people, data, systems, and the wider environment in which a project operates.

### 5.7 Quality and Proportionality

Quality expectations should reflect the project's declared purpose, complexity, maturity, and risk. A prototype need not satisfy every requirement of a production system, but it must not be misrepresented as doing so.

### 5.8 Restraint

Not everything developed needs to be published. Selective publication is part of maintaining the Lab's integrity and protecting third-party and confidential material.

## 6. Research and Development Method

The Lab uses an experiment-driven approach. A typical investigation may follow this sequence:

**Research → Hypothesis → Design → Experiment → Prototype → Evaluation → Documentation → Publication Review**

The sequence is a reference method, not a mandatory linear process. Small experiments may use a lighter process, and projects may repeat or revisit earlier activities when evidence or scope changes.

### 6.1 Research

Identify the subject, investigate relevant approaches, and establish the technical or conceptual context. Distinguish sourced information from assumptions and interpretations.

### 6.2 Hypothesis or Research Question

Define what is being investigated and what the work is intended to demonstrate, test, or understand. Where a hypothesis is used, make it specific enough to guide an experiment.

### 6.3 Design

Select appropriate methods, tools, technologies, inputs, and evaluation criteria. Keep the scope proportionate to the question and available resources.

### 6.4 Experimentation and Implementation

Test the proposed approach under conditions that are sufficiently documented to make the outcome understandable. Implementation may be a script, study, interface, prototype, application, or other suitable artefact.

### 6.5 Evaluation

Assess results against the declared objective or criteria. Depending on the work, evaluation may include functional testing, comparative analysis, performance observation, reliability assessment, usability review, or analysis of failure conditions.

### 6.6 Documentation

Record the purpose, method, relevant implementation decisions, dependencies, observations, results, and limitations. Documentation should be sufficient for the intended audience to understand the work and, where feasible, reproduce its relevant behaviour or findings.

### 6.7 Publication Review

Publication is a separate decision from research activity, technical completion, validation, or deployment. Material must pass the applicable publication review before release through the Lab's public channels.

## 7. Project Categories and Project Records

### 7.1 Project Categories

A project may be described using one or more categories that communicate the nature of the work. Categories are descriptive; they are not lifecycle stages, maturity levels, or publication approvals.

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| Research | Investigation of a technical, methodological, or conceptual question. |
| Experiment | Exploration of a specific method, technology, or hypothesis. |
| Proof of Concept | A limited implementation used to examine the feasibility of an idea. |
| Prototype | An early functional implementation used to explore or evaluate an approach. |
| Tool | An independent utility or reusable technical resource. |
| Framework | A reusable structure, method, or development foundation. |
| Creative Study | An exploration of computational, generative, or interactive creativity. |
| Documentation | A technical specification, method, study, or research reference. |

A category may be omitted or adapted when it does not meaningfully describe the work. A category must not be used to imply a level of testing, readiness, or support.

### 7.2 Required Separation of Project Attributes

Project records must keep the following six attributes separate. They answer different questions and must not be substituted for one another.

| Attribute | Question answered | Canonical source |
|---|---|---|
| Lifecycle Stage | Where is the project in the Lab's workflow? | `PROJECT-LIFECYCLE.md` |
| Project Status | What is the project's current state? | `PROJECT-LIFECYCLE.md` |
| Technical Maturity | How far has the technical work been assessed and validated? | `QUALITY-STANDARDS.md` |
| Operational Readiness | What operating context has the work been assessed for? | `QUALITY-STANDARDS.md` |
| Publication Classification | What level of publication scrutiny or restriction applies? | `PUBLICATION-POLICY.md` |
| Publication Status | What is the current publication review state or recorded decision? | `PUBLICATION-POLICY.md` |

The canonical values are:

**Lifecycle Stage** — `Idea and Intake`; `Proposal`; `Discovery and Feasibility`; `Experimentation and Development`; `Validation and Evaluation`; `Publication Review`; `Publication and Release`; `Maintenance and Evolution`; `Archival or Discontinuation`.

**Project Status** — `Proposed`; `Concept`; `Experimental`; `Prototype`; `Development`; `Validation`; `Published`; `Maintenance`; `Archived`; `Discontinued`.

**Technical Maturity** — `Not Assessed`; `Exploratory`; `Prototype`; `Validated`.

**Operational Readiness** — `Not Assessed`; `Research or Experimental Use Only`; `Demonstration Only`; `Limited Operational Use`; `Production-Oriented`.

**Publication Classification** — `P0`; `P1`; `P2`; `P3`.

**Publication Status** — `PENDING`; `APPROVED`; `REJECTED`; `WITHDRAWN`.

The definitions, criteria, approval requirements, and permitted use of these values are owned by the canonical source documents listed above. This Charter does not redefine them. In particular:

- A lifecycle stage is not a project status.
- A project status is not a technical maturity assessment.
- Technical maturity does not establish operational readiness.
- Neither maturity nor readiness grants publication permission.
- Publication classification is not a maturity level.
- `PENDING` identifies a publication review state; it is not an approval decision.
- A public release or successful demonstration does not, by itself, establish that a project is validated or production-oriented.

Project records should use only the fields relevant to the work and should not invent additional competing status vocabularies.

## 8. Governance and Accountability

The Lab currently operates under a founder-led governance model. J-ART is the founder and maintainer and holds responsibility for the Lab's direction, policies, repository administration, and publication decisions, subject to applicable law, agreements, licences, and platform procedures.

The detailed allocation of authority, delegation, access management, collaboration, conflict handling, continuity, and governance records is defined in `GOVERNANCE.md`.

The current model does not imply the existence of a board, formal research committee, employees, departments, or external supervisory body unless such structures are explicitly established and documented.

Consultation with contributors or collaborators may inform a decision. Consultation does not transfer decision-making authority unless a specific delegation or agreement says otherwise.

## 9. Intellectual Property and Confidentiality

### 9.1 Public and Private Work Must Remain Separate

The public Lab repository is not a mirror, staging area, or distribution channel for private commercial projects, client work, or confidential development environments.

Only material that has an appropriate basis for public release may be included in the Lab's public repositories. A similar general idea or technology does not authorise reuse of another project's protected implementation.

### 9.2 Protected Material

Protected material may include source code, architecture, specifications, datasets, design assets, internal workflows, business logic, private prompts, system configurations, credentials, personal information, and non-public operational details.

Confidentiality risks may also appear in commit history, issue discussions, screenshots, generated files, metadata, examples, diagrams, and demonstrations. Review must consider the complete publication context, not only the visible source files.

### 9.3 Ownership and Permission

The Lab does not claim ownership of third-party material or of contributions merely because they are submitted to a repository. Authorship, ownership, attribution, and permission to use or distribute a contribution must be established through the applicable licence or a separate explicit agreement.

The repository currently has no general open-source licence. Public accessibility must not be interpreted as permission to reproduce, modify, distribute, or commercially exploit original material. Project-specific rights statements or licences must be explicit and limited to the material they cover.

### 9.4 Unclear Rights or Confidentiality

Where ownership, permission, licensing compatibility, or confidentiality cannot be established, publication must be withheld until the issue is resolved. Removing a brand name, renaming a file, or extracting a small fragment does not automatically make protected material safe to publish.

## 10. Publication Principles

Publication under the Lab's identity is intentional, selective, and subject to review.

Before release, the responsible maintainer must ensure that the applicable requirements have been considered, including:

- Clear purpose, scope, and public description.
- Appropriate ownership and publication permissions.
- Review of third-party code, models, datasets, assets, and dependencies.
- Removal of credentials, private data, and confidential information.
- Relevant security, privacy, and safety considerations.
- Accurate project status, maturity, readiness, and limitations.
- Sufficient installation, use, and reproduction information for the declared release.
- Appropriate attribution, licensing, and rights statements.

The detailed classification levels, review workflow, approval record, release requirements, withdrawal conditions, and post-publication responsibilities are governed by `PUBLICATION-POLICY.md`.

No project is approved for publication solely because it is technically complete, publicly accessible, listed in the repository, or marked `Published` in a project record. The actual publication decision must be recorded under the applicable policy.

## 11. Responsible Use of Artificial Intelligence

AI-related work must be developed and described with transparency, critical evaluation, and appropriate human responsibility.

Where AI materially contributes to a project, relevant documentation should identify, as appropriate:

- The model, provider, version, or configuration.
- The purpose and role of the AI component.
- The nature and source of relevant inputs or data.
- Material prompting, retrieval, or orchestration methods where disclosure is authorised.
- The evaluation approach and observed limitations.
- Human review, intervention, or decision-making requirements.
- Relevant external service, cost, latency, privacy, and security dependencies.

AI-generated output must not automatically be treated as correct or verified. Claims about accuracy, reliability, autonomy, fairness, or safety must be supported by evidence appropriate to the claim.

The detailed requirements are established in `RESPONSIBLE-AI.md` and the relevant quality, research, security, and publication policies.

## 12. Technical Quality, Data, and External Dependencies

Projects should follow engineering and quality practices proportionate to their purpose and assessed maturity. Relevant considerations include:

- Understandable architecture and separation of responsibilities.
- Appropriate dependency and configuration management.
- Input validation and error handling.
- Testing and documented evaluation.
- Maintainability, reproducibility, and performance.
- Security, privacy, and accessibility where relevant.
- Clear documentation of limitations and operating requirements.

Projects involving data or external services must identify relevant sources, usage rights, processing conditions, dependencies, and constraints. Personal, sensitive, confidential, or restricted data must not be used or disclosed without an appropriate basis and safeguards.

The detailed technical expectations are defined in `QUALITY-STANDARDS.md`, `RESEARCH-METHODOLOGY.md`, `RESPONSIBLE-AI.md`, and `SECURITY.md`.

## 13. Contributions and Collaboration

The Lab is currently maintained independently by J-ART. External contributions or collaborations may be considered where the scope, responsibilities, rights, and publication conditions are sufficiently clear.

Submitting a contribution does not automatically:

- Transfer ownership of that contribution to the Lab.
- Grant the Lab a licence or other permission beyond the applicable terms.
- Grant repository administration or governance authority.
- Authorise public distribution of the contribution.
- Establish employment, partnership, agency, or joint ownership.
- Authorise a contributor to represent the Lab externally.

Contributors must have the right to submit the material they provide and must disclose relevant third-party dependencies or restrictions. Substantive collaboration involving confidential information, commercial rights, financial commitments, or joint ownership should be documented explicitly before work begins.

The practical contribution workflow and behavioural expectations are defined in `CONTRIBUTING.md` and `CODE-OF-CONDUCT.md`.

## 14. Documentation and Knowledge Preservation

Documentation is part of the Lab's research and engineering method. It should be accurate, understandable, relevant, and proportionate to the scope of the work.

Substantial projects should document their purpose, scope, methods, relevant technologies, dependencies, implementation decisions, evaluation, results, limitations, and publication conditions.

Important findings should not remain only in informal conversations or undocumented development environments when they are intended to inform future work. Records should be preserved in suitable locations, with restricted information kept out of public repositories.

The Lab's project documentation structure is maintained in `PROJECT-TEMPLATE.md`. Research-specific expectations are maintained in `RESEARCH-METHODOLOGY.md`.

## 15. Lifecycle, Maintenance, and Discontinuation

Projects may progress, pause, repeat earlier work, remain experimental, be published, enter maintenance, be archived, or be discontinued. The lifecycle is not assumed to be a single irreversible sequence.

A project may be valuable without being published or maintained indefinitely. A negative feasibility result or a decision to discontinue is a legitimate outcome when supported by the available evidence and recorded appropriately.

The nine lifecycle stages and ten canonical project statuses are defined only in `PROJECT-LIFECYCLE.md`. Maintenance expectations, technical maturity, and operational readiness are addressed by their respective specialist documents.

## 16. Change Management and Charter Review

The maintainer is responsible for reviewing and approving changes to this Charter. Substantive revisions should include a version, date, summary of changes, and reason for revision.

The document uses the following versioning convention:

- **Major version:** A substantial change to the Lab's identity, mission, governance principles, or foundational boundaries.
- **Minor version:** A substantive addition or revision that does not replace the Charter's foundational direction.
- **Patch version:** Editorial corrections, clarifications, and non-substantive improvements.

The current version and effective date must reflect the actual approval and repository record. A draft must not be described as an approved or effective policy.

## 18. Foundational Statement

Framing Creativity Lab exists to explore what becomes possible when creative thinking, technical understanding, and emerging technologies meet.

Its value is not measured only by the number of repositories, prototypes, or tools it produces. It is also measured by the quality of the questions it investigates, the discipline of its experiments, the clarity of its findings, and the responsibility with which it shares its work.

The Lab recognises that meaningful innovation requires both freedom to explore and discipline to evaluate. Its Charter exists to protect that balance.

**Explore without assuming. Experiment without obscuring. Build with intention. Evaluate with rigor. Publish with responsibility.**
